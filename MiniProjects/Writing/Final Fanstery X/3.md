### II. DANCING INCHES FROM DEATH

The first artillery shell, a hundred-pound cast-iron casing packed with pressurized fire-materia, struck Tidus squarely in the left shoulder.

The detonation blew him twenty feet backward into the saturated mud of the Thunder Plains.

The blast tore away three of his bronze cooling fins; his left arm, severed at the bicep by the concussive shockwave, tumbled through the dark air and landed fifteen paces away in a pool of standing water. His dream-stuff matrix flared in blinding, jagged bursts of emerald and violet light as the physical tissue shredded.

On the ridge half a mile away, the Crusader spotters cheered, their brass field glasses reflecting the flash of the burning plains.

Then the sky opened.

A pillar of blue-white planetary lightning, three feet thick and carrying thirty thousand amperes of unconditioned atmospheric potential, struck the ground six inches from Tidus’s head.

The thunder that followed cracked the stone foundations of the nearby lightning rod tower.

Tidus did not die.

His right hand—an intricate, six-fingered claw of articulated bronze struts and salvaged Al Bhed hydraulic servos—shot out of the smoking mud. He grabbed the severed end of his own left arm from the puddle.

He didn't search for a surgeon. He didn't seek cover.

With terrifying, mechanical calm, he slammed the severed arm back against his ruined shoulder socket. A spray of glowing pyreflies erupted from the wound like molten solder. His fingers worked in a blur of frantic, practiced efficiency: he jammed a brass alignment pin through the fractured humerus joint, tightened two hose clamps around the severed hydraulic conduits with his teeth, and shoved the bare copper signal wires back into their terminal strip.

*Click-whirrrrr.*

The fingers of the severed arm twitched. The servos pressurized. Full motor control was re-established in eight seconds.

`DAMAGE AUDIT: CHASSIS LOSS: 14.2% // SENSORY FEEDBACK: COMPROMISED`  
`REPAIRS COMPLETED: FIELD SPLICE APPLIED`  
`ENVIRONMENTAL POTENTIAL: ABNORMALLY RICH // 1.2 GW PER STRIKE`  
`CONCLUSION: THE ENEMY HAS SELECTED AN OPTIMAL RECHARGING BASIN`

The monks' second salvo whistled overhead—six high-explosive mortar rounds descending in a tight spread.

Tidus did not try to outrun the artillery.

He activated the glass cylinder on his back.

A pulse of seventy millivolts surged through Seymour Guado’s preserved spinal cord.

*BZZZZZZZZT.*

In the open space fifty feet in front of him, the air violently tore open. A towering, grotesque silhouette of hard-light dream-matter materialized: a twisted, winged horror cobbled from Valefor’s captured carrier frequency and Anima’s distorted geometry.

It was not a true aeon. It had no mind, no will, and no holy radiance. It was an empty, high-density pyrefly meatshield.

The six mortar rounds slammed directly into the phantom construct.

*BOOM-BOOM-BOOM-BOOM.*

The explosions tore through the hard-light projection in blinding geysers of fire and shrapnel. The phantom absorbed ninety percent of the blast energy before collapsing into gray smoke. Tidus stepped through the falling dirt, completely unscathed, his right claw holding a heavy hydraulic shear.

On the ridge, Commander Marik dropped his field glasses. His jaw hung slack beneath his steel visor.

*"Did that... did that thing just summon an aeon to block a shell?!"*

*"Sir!"* a spotter screamed, pointing toward the plain. *"The lightning! Look at the lightning!"*

### III. THE GROUNDING CRUCIBLE

Tidus was running.

He did not run like a man or a beast. He moved with a horrifying, uneven gallop, his hydraulic legs hissing and venting steam with every stride, his long cooling fins fanned out like the wings of a predatory mantis.

He sprinted directly toward the nearest Yevonite lightning rod—a fifty-foot tower of wrought iron driven deep into the bedrock to attract and ground the plains’ atmospheric fury.

A Crusader crawler cannon—an eight-legged, steam-driven iron behemoth carrying dual twenty-pounder smoothbore cannons—intercepted him at the base of the tower. The crawler's iron plow lowered, charging at twenty miles an hour to crush him against the iron tower.

Tidus leaped.

He didn't jump over the crawler. He slammed directly into the front glacis plate of the charging war-machina.

The impact caved in his own chest plate; two of his artificial ribs snapped with a sound like pistol shots. But his hands did not let go. His steel claws punched through the machine’s thin brass viewing slits, ripping out the pilot's steering linkage in a shower of copper oil lines.

He didn't destroy the crawler. He *field-stripped* it while it was moving.

With savage, industrial speed, Tidus severed the crawler’s main steam supply pipe with his pneumatic shears, redirecting the high-pressure exhaust directly into his own leg thrusters. He unbolted the crawler's twelve-volt secondary battery bank with three wrench strokes, slammed the heavy lead-acid cells into his own hip racks, and spliced the cables into his spinal bus with bare, sparking wires.

Then he reached for the lightning rod tower.

He grabbed the heavy copper grounding cable that ran down the side of the fifty-foot iron spire. He wrapped the bare, three-inch cable twice around his left forearm, anchoring the other end directly into the crawler cannon’s steel chassis.

Above them, the black clouds curdled.

A strike of forty million volts hit the tip of the tower.

The energy surged down the iron spire. But it did not sink harmlessly into the earth. It hit Tidus’s arm.

A mortal body would have been vaporized into carbon dust.

Tidus’s dream-stuff frame ignited in a blinding, incandescent corona of blue-white fire. His waxy skin turned transparent; the internal pyrefly lattice glowed so bright that the skeletons of Seymour’s spine on his back and the Sahagin ganglia in his skull were etched in brilliant, razor-sharp silhouette against the storm clouds.

He threw his head back. From the acoustic synthesizer beneath his chin came a sound that froze the blood of every man on the field:

It was laughter.

Pure, manic, electric laughter that rattled through the Crusaders’ iron breastplates.

He directed the surge.

Using his own body as a high-voltage bus bar, Tidus shunted the forty-million-volt strike through the crawler cannon’s iron tracks and out across the wet, waterlogged surface of the plain.

A sheet of blue lightning rippled across three hundred yards of saturated mud.

The warrior-monks' palisade detonated as the artillery powder stores cook off simultaneously. The iron armor of thirty Crusaders welded itself shut in a blinding flash of arc-welding heat; men dropped like lead weights into the mud, their nervous systems completely shorted by the ground-current wave.

### IV. FIELD-STRIPPING THE APPARATUS

An hour later, the storm had moved north toward the Macalania tree line.

The Thunder Plains were silent, save for the crackle of burning supply wagons and the low, persistent hiss of steam venting from dead machina.

Commander Marik was dragged from the wreckage of his command crawler by a pair of iron claws.

The Commander was missing his helmet. His face was scorched black by electrical burns; his left leg was broken, pinned beneath an iron tread. He looked up, coughing blood, his breath coming in ragged, shallow gasps.

Above him stood the White-Iron Fiend.

It was no longer recognizable as the boy from the Moonflow causeway.

It had grown to nine feet in height. It had incorporated the armored tracks of two Crusader crawlers into its lower torso, creating a heavy, four-wheeled centaur-like chassis that churned through the mud with hydraulic ease. Its chest was reinforced with three layers of bolted boiler plate; on its right shoulder, the barrel of an anti-fiend light cannon had been modified into an auxiliary cooling duct, puffing steady rings of white steam into the rain.

Seymour’s spine, glowing inside its oil-filled cylinder, was humming a low, steady sixty-hertz drone.

Tidus looked down at the Commander.

His blue eye—the only piece of human anatomy remaining on his face—was completely calm. There was no rage in it. No hatred. Only the cold, endless calculation of a machine assessing salvage value.

*"W-What are you...?"* Marik whispered, his voice rattling in his chest. *"You're not a fiend... Yevon have mercy... what are you building?"*

Tidus tilted his head. The auxiliary optic lenses clicked, scanning the Commander's shattered crawler.

*"I am building bandwidth,"* Tidus said. The acoustic synthesizer was smooth now, resonant, echoing off the iron towers like a church bell. *"Your artillery had an energy efficiency of only eleven percent. The propellant was poorly mixed. The rifling was worn down by eighty microns. A shameful waste of carbon and iron."*

He reached down with his hydraulic claw.

He didn't kill the Commander. He simply ripped the brass communication headset from the man's head, cut the microphone wire with his teeth, and shoved the lead into a terminal strip on his neck.

`EXTERNAL SENSOR INTEGRATION: COMPLETE`  
`RADIO BAND: YEVON MILITARY GRID // UNENCRYPTED`  
`NORTHERN TRANSMISSION DETECTED: MT. GAGAZET SECTOR`  
`CARRIER SOURCE: HIGH-DENSITY STANDING WAVE ARRAY (WALL OF THE FAYTH)`

Tidus straightened up.

He ignored the groaning, dying men scattered across the blackened plain. He turned his heavy, track-driven chassis toward the northern mountains, where the jagged peaks of Gagazet pierced the gray sky like broken teeth.

The signal from the north was deafening.

It was not a single aeon. It was thousands of them. A massive, continent-sized antenna broadcasting an infinite, singing dream.

*"The source,"* Tidus murmured. His tracks bit into the mud, throwing sheets of black silt behind him as he accelerated. *"The server room is in the mountains."*
