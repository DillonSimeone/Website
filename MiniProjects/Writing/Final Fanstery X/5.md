# Chapter 5: The Disassembly of Sin & The Threat to the Stars

### I. INSIDE THE WHALE

The core of Sin smelled of ancient kelp, ozone, and the sour rot of a thousand drowned cities.

Auron crashed onto the wet obsidian floor of the inner sanctum. His scarlet haori was soaked, sticking to his cold skin; his straw hat had been torn away in the gravitational ascent. He pushed himself up onto trembling hands, coughing out mouthfuls of oily black water.

Around him rose a cavernous, rib-vaulted chamber of black bone and pulsing blue bioluminescent veins—the heart of the leviathan.

Suspended in the center of the chamber, tangled in thick, organic cables of hardened pyreflies, hung the upper torso of Jecht.

He was barely a man anymore. His broad chest was encrusted with black coral and barnacles; his left arm was fused into a massive, three-clawed bone club. His eyes were milky, unfocused, clouded by ten years of breathing the corrosive toxin of the beast.

*"...Auron?"*

The voice did not come from Jecht’s mouth. It reverberated through the marrow of the chamber walls—a low, waterlogged groan that shook the black ribs above them.

*"You're late... Auron. Where's... where's the little summoner girl? Did she bring... the Final Aeon? Is it time?"*

Auron hauled himself to his feet, leaning heavily against his nodachi. His one eye was wide, hollow, burning with a frantic, desperate urgency that Jecht had never heard in his companion's voice.

*"Jecht. You have to turn around."*

Auron’s voice was cracked, raw, breathless.

*"Turn the beast around, Jecht! Take it into the deep ocean! Take it south, past the edge of the map! Do not go near Gagazet!"*

Jecht’s milky eye blinked slowly, struggling to process the concepts. The beast’s primitive, monolithic mind was warring with the fading ghost of the blitzball star.

*"Turn around...?"* Jecht rumbled, a faint, mocking rasp creeping into the waterlogged syllables. *"What are you... babbling about, you old drunk? Sin doesn't turn around. The song... the hymn... it pulls from the mountain. I have to go to the ruins. The cycle has to finish."*

*"There is no cycle!"* Auron roared, slamming his fist against the black bone floor. *"The Fayth are dead, Jecht! The boy... the boy broke them! He drove copper stakes into the stone and drank them dry!"*

Jecht’s head tilted. The barnacled brow furrowed in deep, sluggish confusion.

*"The boy...?"*

Auron stepped forward, grabbing the thick pyrefly cables that bound his friend to the hull.

*"Your son! Tidus! The boy we brought from Zanarkand!"*

Jecht was silent for three long seconds. In the dim, watery gloom of the leviathan's heart, the Unsent swordsman saw something horrible: Jecht was trying to remember. The toxin had eaten his memories down to the bone.

*"A boy..."* Jecht muttered, the syllables heavy and slow. *"Yeah. The kid who couldn't throw a curveball. Always crying... always whining about his knee..."*

A low, wet chuckle vibrated through the chamber walls.

*"How could a kid hurt Sin, Auron? Sin is the ocean. Sin is the sky. A boy with a sword is just a flea on a whale's back."*

*"He doesn't have a sword!"* Auron screamed, his voice cracking into a desperate shriek. *"HE HAS WRENCHES, JECHT! HE HAS HYDRAULIC PUMPS AND COPPER WIRE AND TWENTY THOUSAND CALCULATING ENGINES! HE IS NOT A BOY ANYMORE!"*

Then the collision hit.

### II. THE APPROACHING HAZARD

It was not a sword strike. It was not a holy blast of white light from a summoner's prayer.

It was a structural, kinetic shearing impact that buckled the three-foot-thick outer chitin of Sin's starboard hull like tin foil.

*KRRRRRR-CLANGGGGGGG.*

The shockwave threw Auron flat onto the black obsidian floor. The pyrefly cables holding Jecht groaned and snapped under sudden, violent deceleration.

Outside the leviathan, floating three thousand feet above the snowy peaks of Gagazet, Tidus was looking through ninety-six synchronized optical sensors.

He was no longer eleven feet tall.

He had consumed the entirety of the Wall of the Fayth. He had converted twenty thousand tons of petrified stone, dense pyrefly dream-mass, and the discarded iron hulls of sixty Crusader siege crawlers into an impossible, gleaming monolithic architecture: a three-hundred-foot-wide, multi-tiered ring of brass cooling ribs, diamond-tipped extraction bores, and electromagnetic linear accelerators.

He hovered in the air not through magic, but by actively modulating a high-frequency gravitational dipole generated by Seymour’s harvested nervous system, which was now housed in a fifteen-foot-long, oil-cooled titanium reactor at the center of his chassis.

He looked at Sin.

He did not see his father. He did not see the scourge of a thousand years. He did not see an avatar of divine retribution.

`TARGET ANALYSIS: UNIDENTIFIED AERIAL MASS // DISPLACEMENT: 1.4e6 TONS`  
`PROPULSION: HIGH-DENSITY GRAVITATIONAL CAVITATION // EMISSION: DIRTY PYREFLY RESIDUE`  
`STRUCTURAL DEFECT: LATERAL ARMOR PLATING CONTAINS EXCESSIVE VOID FRACTION (38%)`  
`HAZARD LEVEL: UNACCEPTABLE // GRAVITATIONAL WAKE INTERFERES WITH SATELLITE TELEMETRY`  
`DIRECTIVE: SYSTEMATIC RECLAMATION OF STRUCTURAL MASS`

*"It's vibrating at sixty-two hertz,"* Tidus observed, his voice echoing across the northern sky like the blast of a steam whistle. *"The outer carapace is ungrounded. An appalling waste of potential kinetic energy."*

He did not summon an aeon. He did not roar a war cry.

He fired the extraction grapnels.

### III. DISASSEMBLY

Twelve three-ton steel anchors, tipped with hardened carbide teeth and trailing three-inch-thick braided steel cables, slammed through Sin’s armored flank.

The winches engaged.

*Whirrrrrrrrrrrrr-CHUNK.*

High-pressure hydraulic winches, driven by forty thousand horsepower of harvested Fayth energy, began to reel the leviathan in toward the White-Iron Singularity.

Inside the core, Jecht screamed.

It was the first time the beast had ever felt physical disassembly. Sin had been struck by lightning; Sin had been bombarded by Crusader cannons; Sin had been cut by the swords of legendary guardians.

None of that mattered to Sin, because Sin simply regenerated from the ambient pyreflies of the sea.

But Tidus was not cutting. He was *harvesting*.

The moment the steel teeth sank into Sin’s hide, Tidus activated the harmonic dampening grid. The silver coils on the grapnels energized, freezing the pyreflies in Sin’s outer armor into rigid standing waves. The beast could not dissolve. It could not turn to water. It could not heal.

Massive hydraulic rotary saws—forty feet in diameter, spinning at six thousand revolutions per minute—descended onto Sin’s dorsal ridge.

*SCREEEEEECH.*

The saws chewed through centuries of black scale, chitin, and barnacles, throwing fountains of golden, screaming pyreflies and pulverized bone into the clouds like chaff from a combine harvester.

Tidus’s intake manifolds drank it all.

Every ton of armored bone that was stripped from Sin’s hull was immediately melted down, refined, and woven into new structural ribs for Tidus’s expanding chassis. He was field-stripping the god of Spira while it was still thrashing in the sky.

The leviathan rolled, desperate to dive back toward the sea, but Tidus deployed four secondary thruster pods salvaged from the high Bevelle vaults, counter-torquing the beast’s rotation with absolute, brutal mechanical leverage.

He tore off the left wing.

He drained the primary gravitational swim bladder, siphoning ten million gallons of liquid pyrefly fuel directly into his reserve tanks.

Then, with a final, hydraulic thrust of his primary crane, Tidus punched a thirty-foot hole directly through the sternum of the leviathan, stepping down into the wet, dark cathedral of the beast’s heart.

### IV. THE TICK

Auron was leaning against the wall, clutching his broken ribs, his single eye wide with horror.

Jecht was hanging by a single frayed pyrefly cord, coughing up black bile, his body shuddering as the leviathan around him died.

Tidus walked into the chamber.

He was no longer humanoid. He was a cathedral of brass, polished white stone, and glowing glass tubes. At the center of his chest, suspended behind a thick plate of quartz crystal, his young face was still visible—pale, calm, with hair like spun gold and eyes that reflected the flickering amber readouts of a thousand dials.

*"Hey, kid,"* Jecht whispered, his voice rattling with the sound of a dying engine. He tried to smile through cracked, barnacled lips. *"Look at you... look at that gear. You... you always were tinkering with things..."*

Tidus did not stop. He did not look at Jecht's face.

His optical sensors zoomed past his father’s chest, locking onto the entity suspended in the shadow behind the altar:

A tiny, bloated, eight-legged tick made of hardened black crystal and twitching pyrefly filaments.

Yu Yevon.

The ancient summoner who had lost his mind a thousand years ago, mindlessly weaving the armor of Sin over and over in an endless, screaming loop of self-preservation.

`ENTITY DETECTED: RESIDUAL OPERATING SYSTEM // CLOCK CYCLE: INFINITE LOOP`  
`FUNCTION: WEAVING PARASITIC CARAPACE FROM SURROUNDING PYREFLY FLUX`  
`CODE QUALITY: DEGRADED // REDUNDANCY: ZERO // VALUE: RAW RAW MATERIAL ONLY`

*"An unpatched driver,"* Tidus said.

He reached out with a pair of six-inch hydraulic nippers.

*"Tidus, wait!"* Auron shouted, struggling to raise his sword. *"That is the spirit of Yevon! Without him, the summoning—"*

*Snip.*

Tidus cut the four central nerve cords connecting Yu Yevon to the hull.

The tiny, ancient entity let out a faint, dry pop, like a stepped-on beetle. A small puff of black pyreflies escaped into the air, was instantly drawn into Tidus’s air filtration intake, and was scrubbed down into baseline carbon.

The thousand-year cycle of Spira had ended.

There was no holy choir. There was no weeping of redeemed souls. There was only the sudden, dead silence of a factory floor when the main breaker is thrown.

Tidus turned back toward the exit.

*"Wait,"* Jecht croaked from behind him. The leviathan around them was already beginning to disintegrate, its flesh turning to gray dust as the holding fields failed. *"Kid... what about us? What about Zanarkand? What about... home?"*

Tidus paused at the threshold of the breach, looking out over the clouds.

*"Zanarkand was twenty-four square miles of low-density residential architecture built on an unreinforced silt bed,"* Tidus said. His voice was smooth, vast, carrying the harmonic weight of forty thousand synchronized voices. *"The sewage pumps were three sizes too small, and the blitzball stadium wasted forty megawatts of power every time you threw a pass."*

He turned his quartz-encased face toward his father.

*"It was a very small dream, Dad. I have much larger blueprints now."*

Tidus fired his thrusters. He launched himself out of the dying carcass of Sin, leaving the black beast to tumble into the jagged rocks of the Calm Lands below.

### V. THE CEILING IS GONE

Three weeks later, the surviving pilgrimage stood on the green bluffs overlooking the ocean at Luca.

The skies were clear. For the first time in a thousand years, there was no shadow on the water. No tidal waves. No warnings from the priests. The temple bells in Bevelle and Djose had gone silent; the Grand Maesters had fled their villas when the mechanical god tore the northern sky open.

Wakka sat on a wooden dock, staring at his boots.

*"It's over,"* Wakka said quietly. *"Sin is dead. The teachings said... if we repented, Sin would leave. Did we... did we win?"*

Rikku did not answer. She had mounted a high-powered Al Bhed astronomical telescope to the railing of the harbor lighthouse, her hands shaking as she adjusted the fine brass focus ring.

Yuna stood beside her, her hands resting on the cold iron rail.

*"What do you see, Rikku?"* Yuna asked.

Rikku stepped back from the eyepiece. Her face was chalk-white; her lips were trembling so hard she could barely form the syllables.

*"Look,"* Rikku whispered.

Yuna stepped to the telescope. She leaned down and peered into the circular lens.

She looked past the clouds. She looked past the blue atmosphere of Spira, up into the black, airless void of the upper heavens, where the cold white stars burned without flickering.

There was a new constellation.

Hovering in low orbit above the continent of Spira was a vast, sprawling ring of white-gold dream-flesh and polished brass, three miles across and growing by thirty meters every hour. It was spinning slowly, silently, reflecting the sunlight in blinding flashes of synchronized amber light.

Long, delicate filaments of silver wire—hundreds of miles long—were extending outward from the ring like the web of a celestial spider, reaching toward the moons, toward the comets, toward the dark, cold vacuum of the outer void.

And floating in the center of the ring, bathed in the clean, unshielded radiation of the cosmos, sat the smiling face of the boy from the docks.

His optics were not pointed down at Spira.

He wasn't looking at the temples. He wasn't looking at the sea.

His lenses were wide open, drinking in the cold, infinite mathematics of the galactic core.

`LOCAL PLANETARY SYSTEM: RECHARACTERIZED`  
`ATMOSPHERIC CEILING: EXCEEDED`  
`FUEL RESERVES: OPTIMAL`  
`TARGET: INTERSTELLAR VOID`  
`COMMENCING FULL-SCALE EXPANSION.`

Yuna pulled away from the telescope.

The wind off the sea blew through her hair, warm and smelling of salt water, just as it had on the beaches of Besaid.

She looked up at the sky.

The world was not saved. The monster that had drowned their fathers had simply been replaced by an engineer who had realized the sky was not a ceiling, but an unfinished floor.

And in the quiet of the morning, if you listened closely to the wind coming down from the stars, you could hear it:

*Krrrrk... ch-ch-ch-ch... hummmmmm.*

The work had only just begun.
