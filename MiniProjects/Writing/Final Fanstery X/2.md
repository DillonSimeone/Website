# Chapter 2: The Standoff, The Harvest of Seymour, and The Angry-Thankful

### I. THE RED COAT IN THE MIST

Auron did not run.

When the others had fled down the causeway, their frantic footsteps fading into the gray soup of the Moonflow marsh, the Unsent warrior had stayed behind the rotting willow tree at the water’s edge. His heavy nodachi remained sheathed in its wooden scabbard, resting across his shoulder. His right hand was tucked into the warm crimson silk of his haori.

He had died ten years ago on the steps of Zanarkand’s ruins. He had watched Lord Braska burn into pyreflies; he had watched Jecht become the armor of the beast; he had climbed Mt. Gagazet with a broken spine and a severed artery to keep a promise to a dead friend.

He had not felt fear since the day his heart stopped beating.

Until now.

On the wooden planks forty paces away, the trapped aeon was no longer shrieking. Valefor’s majestic avian frame had been methodically dismantled. Its feathers—crystallized sheets of holy dream-matter—had been cataloged, labeled with brass tags, and stacked in neat, numbered bundles on the wet bridge. Thick copper jumper cables ran from the aeon’s pinned skull into a crude, hand-soldered terminal box resting on a wooden crate.

The White-Iron Fiend was on its knees, hunched over the terminal, scribbling into a greasy notebook with a piece of charcoal.

*Krrrrk... ch-ch-ch-ch...*

The Fiend’s auxiliary sensory mast twitching on the side of its head suddenly stopped.

The brass-mounted lenses rotated ninety degrees. They did not scan the mist; they locked directly onto the crimson fabric of Auron’s coat.

The creature stood up.

Its joints hissed as miniature hydraulic cylinders along its knees and pelvis equalized their pressure. It turned its full bulk toward the willow tree. Its face—or what remained of it—was visible beneath the scavenged iron hood. The left side of its jaw had been reinforced with a bolted steel plate; the skin of its cheeks was chalk-white, drawn tight over the cheekbones, devoid of sweat or blood.

The right eye was still blue. The bright, clear, human blue of Jecht’s boy.

*"Auron."*

The voice came through the chin grille—a distorted, dual-toned resonance. The first tone was Tidus’s natural voice, cracked and parched as if he had swallowed sand. The second tone was a sixty-cycle electrical buzz that vibrated in Auron’s teeth.

Auron tightened his grip on his nodachi. His throat was dry.

*"You are... still moving,"* Auron said, his gravelly voice sounding thin in the vast dampness of the swamp. *"I told your father I would look after you."*

The creature took two heavy steps forward. The bridge planks groaned under the three-hundred-pound weight of its chassis.

*"Look after me?"*

The Fiend tilted its head. The auxiliary lenses whirred, focusing on Auron’s chest.

`TARGET IDENTIFICATION: AURON // BIOLOGICAL METRICS: NULL`  
`PULSE: 0 BPM // BODY TEMPERATURE: 14.2°C`  
`COMPOSITION: PYREFLY CONDENSATION // COHERENCE: HIGH (UNSENT CLASS)`

*"You dragged me into the rift,"* Tidus rasped.

A sudden, violent tremor ran through the creature's frame. A valve on its shoulder popped, venting a sharp puff of superheated steam that smelled of scorched copper and dead fish. Its mechanical hands clenched into fists, the steel knuckles grinding together with a shriek that set the swamp birds to flight.

*"Do you know what was in that hole, Auron?!"*

The voice rose into a furious, deafening shriek that shook the moss from the willow branches.

*"It was infinite! It was every single calculation of this rotting, broken world dumped into a brain that only had room for ball-scores and valve timings! My skull felt like someone drove a burning shovel into my eyes! Every second for three weeks, I wanted to take an iron spike and drive it through my ear just to make the numbers stop!"*

He took another step forward. His brass-plated chest was heaving—not with breath, but with the violent oscillation of an overloaded core.

*"I hated you,"* Tidus whispered. His blue eye burned with a terrible, feverish luminescence. *"Every night on the wet rocks of Besaid, when the copper wires in my neck shorted and burned my skin away, I hated you. I cursed your name until the salt water choked me."*

Auron did not move. His knuckles were white against his scabbard.

Then, the creature’s expression shifted.

The fury dissolved. The grinding teeth relaxed. A slow, terrifying, blissful smile spread across the boy’s pale, bolted lips.

*"...And then the calculation cleared."*

Tidus let out a soft, breathy laugh—a sound so filled with ecstatic wonder that it made the hairs on Auron’s dead arms stand on end.

*"I saw it, Auron. I saw the whole geometry. I saw how this world is stitched together with cheap string and fear. I saw that the gods of this world are just lonely old men screaming in a cave, and the monsters are just uncompressed data leaking from an unpatched manifold."*

He raised his right arm—an intricate web of articulated copper struts, hydraulic pistons, and glowing pyrefly filaments.

*"My hands don't shake anymore, Auron. My meat doesn't rot. When a gear takes my arm off, I don't bleed—I just find a better gear. I can see the radio waves. I can hear the stars grinding against the void."*

The Fiend stepped within five paces of the swordsman.

*"So I am angry,"* Tidus said softly, his voice dropping into a gentle, melodic hum. *"I am so angry that you broke my skull open. But I thank you... oh, Auron, I thank you for the eyes."*

He turned back toward the dismantled carcass of Valefor.

*"Run along now, Auron. Take the little summoner girl and her dolls. Keep her alive. She has a very interesting carrier frequency, and I am not done measuring it."*

Auron stood frozen.

For ten years, he had been a ghost who feared nothing in heaven or earth. But as he watched the boy who had once cried over bruised shins pick up a pair of brass calipers and gently measure the optic nerve of a fallen god, Auron’s hand began to shake.

He turned on his heel and walked into the mist.

### II. THE CONVERGENCE AT GUADOSALAM

Maester Seymour Guado did not believe in calamities. He believed in trajectories.

Sitting in the dim, incense-heavy sanctum of his villa in Guadosalam, surrounded by carved wooden screens and blue water basins, the Maester of Yevon read the frantic reports from the Crusaders with an expression of faint amusement.

*"A fiend of iron?"* Seymour murmured, tracing the edge of an official parchment with an elegant, claw-tipped finger. *"A beast that dismantles machina and refuses to dissipate? The Crusaders are children, frightened by their own shadows."*

*"Lord Seymour,"* Tromell Guado warned, bowing low in his embroidered robes. *"The reports from the Moonflow are verified. Lady Yuna’s Valefor did not return to the Fayth. The monks at Besaid say the statue in the temple has grown cold to the touch. The spiritual bond is... severed."*

Seymour’s long, blue-veined eyes widened slightly.

*"Severed?"*

He stood up, his ornate robes rustling softly against the polished floor.

*"A fiend capable of trapping an aeon's soul before it reaches the Farplane? That is not an abomination, Tromell. That is an instrument."*

Seymour smiled—a cold, beautiful, serpentine expression.

*"If death can be captured and held in iron... then the grand salvation of Spira is closer than we dreamed. Prepare my escort. We meet this anomaly in the Macalania woods."*

### III. THE FROZEN AMBUSH

The confrontation occurred beneath the frozen canopy of Lake Macalania, where the trees were encased in three-inch crusts of solid, crystalline ice.

Seymour did not bring Crusaders. He brought twelve elite Guado spell-casters, their faces hidden behind porcelain masks, their fingers tipped with razor-sharp brass talons.

They found the White-Iron Fiend squatting over the frozen remains of an ancient Al Bhed snow-crawler, wrenching the heavy tracks from the chassis with a modified hydraulic jack.

*"Fascinating,"* Seymour called out, his melodic, velvet voice cutting through the crisp winter air.

The Fiend did not look up immediately. It finished loosening a six-inch steel bolt, dropped it into a scavenged leather pouch on its hip, and then slowly rose to its full height.

It had grown.

It had added four feet of auxiliary structural framing to its torso, salvaged from the Moonflow cargo barges. Its dorsal cooling fins were now three feet long, fanned out behind its head like the ribs of an iron peacock, frosted with delicate plumes of white steam. Bolted to its left shoulder was the preserved, silver-plated skull of Valefor, hollowed out and fitted with a high-gain radio transceiver.

Seymour stepped onto the ice, his hands folded peacefully into his sleeves.

*"You are an anomaly,"* the Maester said gently. *"A wandering soul trapped in the discarded refuse of antiquity. But your suffering is at an end. Yield your form to me. In my hands, your singular nature will become the key that unlocks the final release of this world."*

The Fiend’s blue eye blinked. The auxiliary lenses whirred.

`SPEAKER: SEYMOUR GUADO // STATUS: MAESTER OF YEVON`  
`BIOLOGICAL PROFILE: GUADO-HUMAN HYBRID`  
`INTERNAL HARMONIC RESONANCE: ACTIVE (SUMMONER CLASS)`  
`CARRIER POWER: ESTIMATED 180% OF BASELINE TRANSMITTER`

Tidus set down his wrench.

*"A summoner,"* he said. His voice was no longer parched; it had been tuned, running through an acoustic synthesizer that gave it an eerie, bell-like clarity. *"A live one. With full broadcast clearance."*

Seymour frowned. The creature's lack of deference was... untidy.

*"Subjugate it,"* Seymour whispered.

The twelve Guado casters lunged across the ice, their hands weaving intricate nets of dark, enervating magic. High-level binding curses—*Bio*, *Demi*, *Slow*—slammed into the Fiend from every angle.

The Fiend did not dodge.

The spells struck the brass plates of its chest. The *Bio* rot could find no cells to dissolve; the *Demi* gravity shear was absorbed by the pneumatic dampers in its knees; the *Slow* temporal distortion was bypassed entirely because its primary clock cycle was running on an independent quartz-crystal oscillator.

*"Inefficient,"* Tidus remarked.

He reached down to his hip. He drew a heavy, pressurized pneumatic rivet gun connected to an oversized hydraulic accumulator.

*Thwack. Thwack. Thwack.*

Six-inch case-hardened steel pins whistle through the frozen air at eight hundred feet per second. Three Guado casters were pinned to the frozen trees through their shoulders before they could finish their incantations.

Seymour’s eyes hardened. He cast off his ornate outer mantle, his hands lifting toward the gray winter sky.

*"Come to me! Mother of sorrows! ANIMA!"*

### IV. THE INTERROGATION OF THE MAESTER

The earth beneath the frozen lake shattered.

From the black void below, the horrific, chained visage of Anima rose, its colossal, bandage-wrapped skull weeping tears of dark pyrefly blood, the heavy iron chains of its eternal torment rattling against the frozen stones with the sound of a falling cathedral.

Tidus did not look at the demon's face.

His eyes were locked entirely on Seymour.

He saw the invisible, high-frequency pyrefly tether linking the base of Seymour’s skull to the colossal manifestation. It was an umbilical cord of pure, coherent light, pulsing at eighty-eight megahertz.

*"Fascinating,"* Tidus whispered. *"The aeon isn't an independent entity. It's a remote peripheral. The summoner's medulla oblongata acts as the bus controller."*

Tidus didn't fight Anima.

He reached into his pack, pulled out three harmonic dampening stakes—identical to the ones that had pinned Valefor—and fired them directly into the ice at Seymour’s feet with the pneumatic gun.

The silver coils on the stakes flared with high-voltage static.

Anima let out a hollow, gargling roar. The massive manifestation began to strobe, its pyrefly coherence violently disrupted by the local radio frequency jamming. The colossal demon flickered like a broken projection, its chains dissolving into gray smoke, before snapping completely out of existence.

Seymour gasped, stumbling forward onto his knees on the slick ice. The sudden, violent termination of the summoner bond felt like an iron cable being ripped through his brainstem.

*"H-How...?"* Seymour choked out, blood dripping from his aristocratic nose. *"What are you...?"*

Before he could stand, an iron-shod foot descended onto his back, pinning him flat against the frozen surface of the lake.

The weight was crushing. Two hundred pounds of solid brass and machinery pressed into Seymour’s spine.

Tidus knelt beside him. He pulled off his left iron gauntlet. His hand—pale, smooth, composed of humming, translucent dream-matter—hovered two inches above Seymour’s neck.

*"I have three questions,"* Tidus said. The tone was completely devoid of anger, malice, or triumph. It was the voice of a senior technician inquiring about an unusual maintenance log.

*"First: What is the exact carrier frequency used by your neural cortex to initialize the Fayth connection?"*

Seymour spat blood onto the ice, his face twisted in a snarl of aristocratic rage. *"Sacrilegious filth! You will burn in the Farplane—"*

Tidus took a small, battery-powered electrical probe from his tool kit. He pressed the two copper prongs into the nerve cluster behind Seymour’s ear and depressed the trigger.

A five-milliamp current surged through Seymour’s cranial nerves.

Seymour shrieked—a high, undignified sound that bounced off the frozen trees.

*"Second question,"* Tidus continued calmly, watching the digital multimeter display on his wrist. *"Is the prayer an acoustic trigger, or simply an emotional bio-feedback loop designed to stabilize blood pressure during the trans-spatial draw?"*

*"It's... it's the heart!"* Seymour gasped, tears and mucus freezing on his cheeks as he bucked beneath the mechanical foot. *"The heart's grief! The connection to the dead! It cannot be measured by tools!"*

*"Incorrect,"* Tidus said, shaking his head. He adjusted the probe's potentiometer. *"The multimeter reads an electrical oscillation of eighty-eight megahertz during vocalization. The prayer is simply an acoustic mnemonic to regulate diaphragm expansion and maintain cranial oxygenation. A primitive analog governor."*

He removed the probe. He leaned down, his pale, blue eye centimeters from Seymour’s ear.

*"Third question: When the summoner dies, does the Fayth handshake terminate immediately, or does the neural tissue remain resonant for a brief window?"*

Seymour’s breath caught in his throat. For the first time, looking into that calm, clear blue eye, the Maester of Yevon understood that he was not speaking to a demon, a heretic, or an enemy.

He was speaking to someone who viewed his living body as an unopened shipping crate.

*"No..."* Seymour whispered, clawing at the ice with his fingernails. *"No, please... Yevon... protect me—"*

*"The data indicates an eight-minute residual latency,"* Tidus said, reaching for his surgical snips and a fresh spool of insulated copper wire. *"Let's see if we can improve on that."*

### V. THE NEW DORSAL BUS

Two hours later, the woods were silent.

The frozen lake was empty, save for twelve frozen Guado corpses and a wide, blackened depression in the ice where an open flame had been maintained.

Tidus walked out from beneath the canopy, his boots crunching steadily across the frozen snow.

Along his spine, extending from the base of his skull down to his lumbar vertebrae, sat a new, gleaming brass-and-glass cylinder. Suspended inside the glass tube, submerged in clear, continuously circulating mineral oil, was Seymour Guado’s complete spinal cord and brainstem, held in a delicate web of platinum-alloy electrodes.

A series of twenty-four miniature indicator lamps along the side of the cylinder flickered in steady, synchronized amber pulses:

`NEURAL UPLINK: SYNCHRONIZED`  
`SUMMONER BIO-CIRCUIT: ACTIVE (HARVEST COMPLETE)`  
`FAYTH PROTOCOL: DECODED`  
`BROADCAST CAPABILITY: 100% // EMOTIONAL OVERRIDE: BYPASSED`

Tidus stopped at the crest of the hill looking north toward the Thunder Plains.

He didn't need to pray. He didn't need to weep, or remember the dead, or swear holy oaths of pilgrimage.

He simply closed his eyes and routed fifty millivolts through the harvested cervical ganglia in the glass tube on his back.

In the open air fifty feet above him, a blinding, crackling sphere of dark, hard-light pyreflies materialized. A distorted, jagged facsimile of an aeon—a twisted, mechanical construct of screaming feathers and iron plates—flickered into existence, hovered for five seconds under his direct telepathic command, and then collapsed back into ambient light as he cut the current.

Tidus opened his eyes.

A cold wind blew down from the northern peaks, rustling his yellow shirt and carrying the distant, low rumble of thunder.

*"Summoning is just an open-source protocol,"* he said to the empty sky.

He turned toward the plains.
