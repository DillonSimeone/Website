# Chapter 4: The Wall of the Fayth & The Soul-Stakes

### I. RIKKU'S AUDIT

The campfire in the ice cave beneath Gagazet crackled with damp birch wood, but none of the warmth reached the five figures huddled against the frozen stone.

Rikku sat with her knees pulled tight to her chest, surrounded by scraps of twisted iron, copper wire, and a shattered piece of hydraulic valve she had recovered from the Thunder Plains battlefield. Her fingers were raw from the cold, smudged with black graphite and dried machine oil.

*"It’s not an Al Bhed machine,"* Rikku said. Her voice was shaking, devoid of its usual manic bounce. *"And it’s not an ancient relic from the Machina War. I’ve looked at every archive on the airship. Nobody ever built circuits like this."*

Wakka leaned against his halberd, his eyes bloodshot, his yellow coat torn and frozen at the hem.

*"It's just a fiend with armor, Rikku,"* Wakka rasped, though there was no conviction left in his words. *"A high-class fiend that ate some machina from a ruined temple. We kill it like any other monster."*

*"You don't understand, Wakka!"* Rikku snapped, jumping up, her brass goggles rattling against her forehead. She held up the piece of copper valve. Her spiral eyes were wide with panic. *"Look at the threading! It's hand-turned! The pitch is twelve microns! It's self-correcting! When the metal got hit by lightning, the copper didn't melt—it expanded into a heat-pipe lattice! It learned how to ground itself mid-battle!"*

She turned to Yuna, who sat silently on a frozen bedroll, her staff resting across her lap like a dead branch.

*"Lady Yuna... Sin destroys things. Sin drowns cities, crushes boats, and leaves people crying. But Sin doesn't build anything. Sin doesn't think."*

Rikku dropped the copper scrap. It landed on the icy stone with a hollow, sickening clink.

*"This thing is growing. Every fight, it gets heavier. Every temple it strips, its clock speed doubles. If this thing reaches the northern summits... it’s not going to replace Sin, Yuna. It’s going to make Sin look like a falling rock."*

Lulu looked up from her moogle doll. Her eyes were sunken, shadowed with exhaustion.

*"Auron,"* Lulu said softly. Her voice was steady, but her fingers were trembling. *"You knew it. On the causeway. When it spoke... it used your name. Who is it?"*

Auron sat across the fire. His red coat was dusted with frozen frost; his straw hat was pulled low over his scarred eye. For a long time, the only sound was the howling of the blizzard outside the cave entrance.

He reached into his robe, pulled out a small porcelain jug of sake, and took a slow, deliberate draught.

*"His name is Tidus,"* Auron said.

Wakka blinked. *"Tidus? What kind of name is that? What temple did he come from?"*

*"He did not come from a temple,"* Auron replied, his gaze locked on the dancing orange embers. *"Ten years ago, Lord Braska and Jecht gave their lives to defeat Sin. Jecht did not die. Jecht became the core of the next beast. Before he was taken, he made me promise one thing: that I would go to the place he came from, find his son, and look after him."*

*"A son?"* Yuna whispered, her hand moving to her throat. *"Jecht had a child?"*

*"In Zanarkand,"* Auron said.

Wakka let out a bitter, disbelieving laugh. *"Zanarkand has been dead for a thousand years, Auron! Nothing lives in the ruins except fiends and ghosts!"*

*"Not the ruins,"* Auron said, his single eye rising to meet Yuna’s gaze. *"The Dream. The Fayth atop this mountain... thousands of dead summoners from the ancient war, sleeping in the stone, dreaming a living city into existence. Jecht was made of their dream. And the boy... the boy was made of their dream."*

The cave went dead quiet. Even the wind seemed to hush.

*"He wasn't an abomination,"* Auron murmured, his voice cracking with an old, dry grief. *"He was just a young mechanic. He smelled of grease and complained about the pumps. But when Sin brought him across the rift... the transition broke his mind. He saw the architecture of the world. And because his body is made of dream-matter instead of mortal flesh... he realized he does not have to obey the laws of death."*

Auron stood up, his nodachi resting across his shoulder.

*"He is coming here. Not for Yuna. Not for Sin. He is coming because this cliff is the antenna that broadcasts his soul. And he wants the transmitter."*

### II. THE SOUL-STAKES

The Wall of the Fayth was a monumental, sheer cliff of black granite rising two thousand feet into the blizzard of upper Gagazet.

Carved into the face of the mountain were thousands upon thousands of human silhouettes—the petrified, stone-encased bodies of the ancient Zanarkand summoners, their eyes closed in eternal slumber, their collective mouths parted in a continuous, silent, telepathic hymn that vibrated through the bedrock like the hum of a continental power station.

The sky above the ridge was pitch black, illuminated only by the cascading curtains of aurora borealis that spilled from the mountain’s crown.

Yuna stood on the snow-covered plateau before the wall, flanked by Kimahri, Wakka, Lulu, and Rikku.

*"Lady Yuna!"* Kimahri barked, his ears flattening against his skull.

Through the whiteout of the blizzard, the sound had returned.

*Krrrrk... ch-ch-ch-ch... hummmmmm.*

It did not walk on legs.

The White-Iron Fiend was now eleven feet tall and fifteen feet long—a massive, six-wheeled iron dreadnought. Its chassis was constructed from the heavy armor plates of three Crusader crawlers, bolted together with six-inch steel rivets. Steam hissed continuously from twin radiator towers mounted behind its cab; on its front glacis, like a demonic figurehead, hung the silver-plated skull of Valefor, its hollow eye sockets glowing with cold blue coherent pyreflies.

Suspended along its dorsal spine, Seymour Guado’s preserved spinal column was glowing a violent, electric violet, pulsing in synchronization with the hum of the mountain.

The machine rolled to a halt twenty paces from the Wall.

Its driver—the upper torso of Tidus, his pale skin stitched with heavy copper cables, his left arm a massive hydraulic crane tipped with a three-pronged diamond-tipped drill—turned its head toward the party.

His blue eye blinked through a haze of frozen condensation.

*"Lady Yuna,"* Tidus said. His acoustic synthesizer was crystal-clear now, ringing like a bell across the frozen plateau. *"You have made excellent time. Your walking speed averages four point two miles per hour across mountainous terrain. A commendable output for biological bipedal units."*

*"Tidus!"* Yuna cried out, stepping forward into the blizzard, her staff raised. Tears froze on her cheeks. *"Listen to me! Auron told us who you are! You're not a monster! You're Jecht’s son! You're a human being!"*

The Fiend paused.

The auxiliary lenses on his temple whirred, zooming in on Yuna’s face, then shifting down to her chest, measuring her respiratory volume and heartbeat.

*"A human being?"*

Tidus let out a soft, pitying click of his tongue.

*"Why would you insult me like that, Yuna?"*

He extended his left crane arm. With a terrifying shriek of pneumatic pressure, he drove a four-inch case-hardened steel stake, wrapped in thick spools of superconducting silver wire, directly into the chest of one of the petrified Fayth statues carved into the mountain wall.

*CRACK.*

The stone cracked. From the puncture wound in the ancient stone statue, a torrent of liquid pyreflies—dense, luminescent, emerald-and-gold dream-stuff—erupted like high-pressure crude oil from a ruptured well.

The liquid light did not spray onto the snow. It was sucked down through the hollow core of the steel stake, through the silver coils, and directly into the intake manifolds of Tidus’s chassis.

His dream-stuff flesh responded instantly.

Under the skin of his chest, the pyrefly lattice expanded, thickening, snapping into new, denser crystalline matrices. The bolted iron plates on his shoulders shifted, re-seating themselves as new hydraulic muscle fibers—spun from pure, condensed spiritual light—grew across his chassis like glowing vines.

He threw his head back, letting out a long, shuddering sigh of pure, physical ecstasy.

*"Do you know what a human being is, Yuna?"* Tidus asked, his voice melodic, calm, terrifyingly reasonable. *"A human being is an inefficient, leaking sack of salted water, fragile lipids, and unstable amino acids. You require three thousand calories a day just to avoid muscular atrophy. You take eighty years to decay, and if a rusty nail punctures your skin, your white blood cells panic and you die of fever in the dark."*

He drove a second stake into another statue. Another geyser of golden light flooded into his spine. Seymour’s preserved nervous system flared with blinding brilliance.

*"Even your fiends are pathetic,"* Tidus continued, gesturing with his crane toward the mountains. *"Fiends are just emotional garbage—the undigested panic of dead people who couldn't let go of their shoes or their daughters. Chaotic, noisy, unindexed data."*

He looked down at his own hand. He flexed his six mechanical fingers. Beneath the skin, the pyreflies pulsed with the steady, clean rhythm of an atomic clock.

*"But this... dream-matter? It is pure, programmable mass. It has no cellular limits. It has no biological decay. If I need more memory, I do not study for twenty years—I add six registers and solder a new bus. If I need more leverage, I do not train—I increase the piston bore by thirty millimeters."*

He smiled—a wide, bright, terrifyingly innocent smile that belonged entirely to the boy from the Zanarkand docks.

*"I am not falling apart, Yuna. I am finally getting large enough to breathe."*

*"You're destroying the Fayth!"* Wakka screamed, raising his blitzball. *"Those are holy people! They're praying for our world!"*

*"They are not praying,"* Tidus replied calmly. *"They are running an infinite loop on a dead server. I am simply optimizing the throughput."*

He turned back to the cliff face.

From his lower chassis, twelve more pneumatic rams extended, driving heavy copper stakes deep into the granite wall. The entire mountain began to shake. The silent hymn of the Fayth rose into a high, piercing electronic shriek as twenty thousand sleeping souls were channeled directly into the dreadnought’s expanding computational core.

`SYSTEM LOAD: 4.8e15 FLOPS // BANDWIDTH: EXPONENTIAL`  
`LOCAL REALITY DRIFT: +14.2% // GRAVITATIONAL ANOMALY DETECTED`  
`MASS ACQUISITION RATE: 140 TONS/HOUR`

### III. THE FLIGHT INTO SIN

Auron did not draw his sword.

He stood twenty paces back, watching the boy absorb the mountain. He watched the massive iron dreadnought double in size, its steel plates warping and fusing into an impossible, gleaming monolithic architecture of white-gold dream-flesh and polished brass.

He saw Wakka charge; he saw Lulu throw her spells; he saw Kimahri strike with his lance.

None of it reached the boy. The kinetic and magical energy of their attacks was absorbed by the swirling vortex of pyreflies three yards before impact, converted directly into auxiliary fuel for the stakes.

*"It's over,"* Auron said quietly.

*"Auron!"* Yuna cried out, her eyes pleading through the storm. *"We have to stop him! The Final Summoning—"*

*"There is no Final Summoning, Yuna,"* Auron said, his voice flat, devoid of hope. *"The Fayth are no longer yours. They belong to the engineer now."*

Auron turned his back on the plateau.

He sprinted toward the southern cliff edge, where the clouds above the Calm Lands were curdling into a violent, spinning hurricane of black water and gravitational distortion.

In the center of the hurricane, rising from the mist like an armored moon, was Sin.

Jecht had arrived.

Auron did not wait. He stepped off the edge of the thousand-foot precipice, plunging directly into the screaming gravitational slipstream of the beast.

His Unsent body, held together only by sheer will and memory, surrendered to the vortex. The gravity well of Sin seized him, pulling him up through the boiling clouds, through the thick, armored hide of the leviathan, dragging him down into the abyssal, drowned cathedral at the core of the monster.
