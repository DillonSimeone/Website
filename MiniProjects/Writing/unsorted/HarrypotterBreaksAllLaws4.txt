The Sorting Hat did not sing. The second its frayed brim grazed Harry Potter’s messy black hair, the ancient leather stiffened, shrieked a sound like tearing sheet metal, and detonated into a pillar of roaring violet fire.

Ash rained down over the three-legged stool. Professor McGonagall stumbled backward, her spectacles cracked from the concussive heat, her hand flying to her throat. Across the Great Hall, four hundred students froze.

Harry didn’t blink. His eleven-year-old feet remained planted squarely on the stone dais, dead center between the staff table and the house tables. He didn't draw a wand. He didn't need one anymore.

Statute 1, Section A of the International Confederation of Wizards: The International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy (1689).

A pulse of silent, nauseatingly dense magic rippled out from Harry’s chest, punching through the castle walls and racing across the curvature of the Earth at the speed of light. Instantly, in every Muggle city from Tokyo to London, the sky turned the color of bruised meat and began projecting thirty-mile-high, high-definition illusions of the Ministry of Magic’s internal archives, dragon reserves, and Azkaban cells, complete with booming multi-language audio commentary explaining how to brew basic potions using household bleach and garden weeds.

Statute 2, Section A: The Unforgivable Curses (1717).

Before Dumbledore could even half-rise from his golden throne, three thousand microscopic arcs of green, red, and purple light licked out from Harry’s fingernails. He didn't kill the students—the statute specified the use against a human being, and the precise legal order demanded he fulfill the criteria in descending severity across the registered population registries. A pinpoint Imperius compelled Cornelius Fudge, currently sitting in his office in Whitehall, to tap-dance until his shins fractured. A localized Cruciatus struck a single nerve ending in Lucius Malfoy’s left pinky toe for 0.04 seconds. An Avada Kedavra routed through a sympathetic spatial tether, instantly snuffing out the life of a comatose Death Eater on life support in St. Mungo’s fourth floor, followed one microsecond later by Quirinus Quirrell’s turban bursting into dust as the parasitic soul clinging to the back of his skull was violently wrenched out, stuffed into a conjured teapot, and legally classified as an unregistered dark artifact.

"Harry!" Dumbledore’s voice thundered, drawing the Elder Wand. "Cease this at—"

Statute 14, Sub-clause 9: High Treason and Assault upon the Supreme Mugwump.

Dumbledore’s beard braided itself into a Victorian corset and slammed him back into his chair, sealing his lips with permanent sovereign glue while a localized temporal distortion aged his boots by four thousand years.

Severus Snape lunged forward, his wand slashing through the air to cast a nonverbal Sectumsempra. He didn't finish the syllable. Harry was already on Statute 412: Unlicensed Transfiguration of Ministry Personnel into Regulated Livestock, turning four incoming Aurors who had just Apparated into the Entrance Hall into prizewinning Yorkshire pigs, followed immediately by Statute 413: Illegal Cross-Breeding of Experimental Monsters, which fused those same pigs with the Giant Squid’s left tentacles via long-range spatial splicing.

It took four hours to reach the bottom of the legal code.

The Great Hall became the epicenter of an apocalyptic administrative nightmare. Aurors, Hit Wizards, Unspeakables, and eventually an entire strike team from MACUSA and the French Ministère des Affaires Magiques tried to breach the doors. None of them got within fifty feet of the stool. Harry didn't look at them. His small face was slack, his green eyes glassy with the profound, bottomless apathy of someone watching a loading bar crawl across a screen for the billionth time.

He broke the Goblin Rebellion treaties by remotely transfiguring every vault door in Gringotts into marshmallow fluff. He violated the Ban on Experimental Breeding by giving every common pigeon in Greater London the venom glands of a Basilisk and the temperament of a Hungarian Horntail. He worked his way down through felony arson, grand theft of sovereign monuments—the Eiffel Tower was briefly swapped with the astronomy tower—all the way down to the petty municipal bylaws of 1974.

At 11:42 PM, amid the smoking ruins of the Great Hall’s enchanted ceiling, while seventy-two immobilized Aurors floated upside down in the air slowly being force-fed illegally imported Dirigible Plums (Bylaw 8,819: Forced Ingestion of Uninspected Produce), Harry hit the very bottom of the register.

Bylaw 9,904, Subsection Z: Charming a Muggle Parking Meter to Display 'Free' on a Bank Holiday.

In Piccadilly Circus, a single meter dinged.

Then, without taking a breath, Harry reversed the list.

From bottom to top. Every municipal infraction, every customs violation, every dark ritual, every war crime, marching back up the mountain of jurisprudence. Outside the castle walls, the world had stopped functioning. The Muggle governments were at DEFCON 1, screaming into red phones as their nuclear silos were harmlessly transfigured into giant ceramic teapots (Statute 88: Mass Alteration of Non-Magical Military Infrastructure). The ICW had collapsed three hours ago after their emergency bunker in Geneva was flooded with four million gallons of unregistered Felix Felicis, causing the delegates to luck themselves into surviving a simultaneous outbreak of Dragon Pox and Spattergroit that Harry triggered two minutes later.

By dawn, the resistance stopped.

There was no one left to fight. The Unspeakables lay in a tangled, groaning heap near the Gryffindor table, their time-turners melted into slag. Snape was embedded waist-deep in the stone floor, legally classified as a load-bearing structural pillar without a building permit. Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger were huddled under the remains of the Hufflepuff table, clutching each other in catatonic terror as the air above them crackled with the residual ozone of ten thousand simultaneous blood curses being cast on uninhabited rocks in the Hebrides.

At 8:00 AM sharp, the sky outside returned to its normal, dreary Scottish grey. The final, topmost crime—the total erasure of the Statute of Secrecy—finished its second cycle as every television and radio on the planet clicked off.

Silence fell over the devastated Great Hall, broken only by the drip of melted silver from the floating candlesticks and the quiet, rhythmic wheezing of an exhausted Minerva McGonagall.

Harry Potter finally blinked. He rolled his narrow shoulders, letting out a small pop in his neck.

Slowly, he raised one small finger, pointed it directly at Albus Dumbledore, and curled it twice, beckoning him over. The sovereign glue on Dumbledore’s mouth vanished, along with the transfigured corset.

Dumbledore’s legs shook so violently he had to use the ruined edge of the lectern to drag himself forward. The greatest wizard of the age looked every single one of his hundred and ten years. His half-moon spectacles were gone, his robes were singed, and his eyes were wide with a primal, childlike horror. He stopped at the base of the dais, looking up at the eleven-year-old boy who had just outperformed Grindelwald, Voldemort, and Herpo the Foul combined before breakfast.

"Harry..." Dumbledore’s voice was a dry rasp. "Why? In the name of mercy... why?"

"I needed you to listen," Harry said. His voice was still a child’s alto, flat and utterly devoid of inflection. "In the other runs, when I just tell you, you twinkle at me. Or you send me to Madam Pomfrey. Or the Unspeakables lock me in the Department of Mysteries for forty years to study my core. Doing the full legal code in order, front to back, without moving my feet, takes a lot of setup. You know you have to account for continental drift when you're aiming a transcontinental hex at a parking meter in Oslo? You can't fake that kind of math."

From beneath the rubble near the doors, the Chief Warlock’s scribe, a young wizard named Percy—no, an older man, Rufus Scrimgeour, who had crawled in around 3:00 AM—stared up with bloodshot eyes. "You broke the world," Scrimgeour whispered. "There are twelve thousand basilisk-pigeons nesting in Big Ben. The global economy is gone."

"Yeah, well, now nobody’s doubting my credentials," Harry said, rubbing his forehead. "Listen closely, because I’m not repeating this. I’m bored. This is my five hundred and ninety-one million, five hundred and twelve thousand, and seventy-ninth loop."

A dead, suffocating quiet hung in the hall.

Snape stopped trying to pull his legs out of the flagstones. Near the front of the first-years, Hermione Granger’s mouth opened, her brain visibly short-circuiting as she tried to process a number that large multiplied by a human lifespan.

"Five hundred..." Dumbledore breathed, the color draining entirely from his face. "My boy, that is... billions of years."

"Tell me about it," Harry sighed. "I’ve saved the world four hundred million times. I’ve conquered it eighty million times. I spent twelve million loops learning how to bake the perfect sourdough using only wandless heat charms. I married everyone legal at some point, lived as a badger in Wales for six thousand years, and pushed Voldemort into the sun so many times I can do it from Privet Drive with a skipped stone. Nothing resets it. Dying doesn't stop it—I just wake up on the train, or in the cupboard, or right here under the hat, depending on how the anchor feels that day. The only thing that changes is me."

Harry finally stepped off the dais. His shoes crunched on the glass and ash. He walked over to the Gryffindor table, where a single platter of roast beef sandwiches and a pitcher of pumpkin juice had miraculously survived the localized reality-warping. He stacked three sandwiches onto a napkin and poured himself a goblet.

"So here’s the deal," Harry said, taking a bite and chewing thoughtfully while three hundred traumatized children and the battered elite of the wizarding world watched him in petrified silence. "Next Monday, at 9:00 AM sharp, I’m going to stand on that spot again. And I’m going to break every single law on the books, top to bottom, bottom to top, including any new ones you pass between now and then. And I’m going to do it every week after that until one of you figures out how to actually, permanently kill me."

He picked up the pitcher of pumpkin juice under one arm.

"You’ve got seven days. Pool your resources with the Muggles, call in the demons, unseal the Death Chamber, whatever. Good luck."

Harry gave a lazy two-fingered salute, walked out the massive double doors of the Great Hall, and headed toward the kitchens to find some treacle tart.

The next seven days were the most frantic, unified period in human history.

With the Statute of Secrecy already obliterated, the British Ministry, the ICW, and the United Nations formed an emergency coalition within twelve hours. Voldemort’s remaining wraith, having been forcefully extracted from Quirrell and handed over to the Unspeakables in a mason jar during Hour Two of Harry’s rampage, was aggressively interrogated for esoteric soul-magic alongside Nicholas Flamel, who arrived via Concord jet from Paris by Tuesday afternoon.

"He is bound by a self-imposed compulsion," Hermione Granger said on Thursday.

She was eleven years old, hadn't slept in sixty hours, and was standing on a folding chair in the middle of the Department of Mysteries, pointing at a chalkboard covered in frantic diagrams. Surrounding her were Dumbledore, Flamel, Croaker—the head Unspeakable—and four constitutional lawyers from the Wizengamot. Because she had been ten feet away from the dais and possessed a photographic memory for rules, she was the only one who had recognized the exact sequence of his spellwork.

"He wasn't casting randomly," Hermione explained, her voice shrill with caffeine and terror. "He was following the 1991 Wizengamot Consolidated Legal Index in strict numerical order! Look—at 1:14 AM he transfigured Professor Snape’s kneecaps into Cornish Pixies, which is Statute 1,044, right after Statute 1,043, which was the unauthorized levitation of Stonehenge! He has to follow the lawbook!"

Croaker’s eyes gleamed behind his spectacles. "A gamified parameters restriction. His mind, after half a billion iterations, has latched onto an external arbitrary rule-set to maintain sanity. If we pass an Emergency Decree—"

"We make a law that kills him the moment he violates it," Snape snarled, leaning heavily on a cane after finally being chiseled out of the floor. "No—better. We make a law that requires his own magic to kill him in order to violate it."

By Sunday night, the Wizengamot had passed Emergency Statute 000-Alpha, placing it at the absolute top of the legal registry.

The wording was drafted by the sharpest legal minds in both worlds and triple-checked by neurosurgeons and curse-breakers: It is henceforth a Class-Zero Felony for any wizard named Harry James Potter to channel, cast, or manifest magical energy without first maintaining an intact, un-liquefied, and un-detonated brainstem, heart, and cerebral cortex.

To break the law, Harry would have to cast magic while simultaneously exploding his own brainstem, vaporizing his heart, and liquefying his gray matter.

On Monday morning at 8:59 AM, the Great Hall was evacuated except for magical viewing sensors and Dumbledore, who refused to abandon his post behind a layer of twelve interlocking blood-wards. Harry strolled in wearing grey muggle sweatpants and a slightly oversized Chudley Cannons jumper he’d nicked from Ron’s trunk, eating a green apple.

He stepped onto the dais. He tossed the apple core aside.

"Let's see what you added," Harry muttered, closing his eyes to scan the ambient legal magic of the Ministry’s ledger. His eyebrow twitched. "Oh, cute. Statute Triple-Zero. Self-inflicted neurological liquidation prior to casting."

At 9:00:00 AM, a wet, muffled pop echoed from inside Harry’s skull, followed by a dull thump in his chest.

Through the diagnostic spells projecting onto the walls of the Ministry bunker, three hundred scientists and Unspeakables watched in horror as Harry’s brainstem, cerebral cortex, and heart instantly vaporized into supercritical steam. By every biological and magical metric, he was instantly, catastrophically dead.

His corpse didn't even sway.

Without a brain to process thought or a heart to pump blood, the raw, calcified reflex of five hundred and ninety-one million loops took over. His nervous system had been redundant for eons; his magic existed as a self-sustaining geometric proof anchored to his physical coordinates. While his skull was still technically full of boiling mist, his left hand twitched—violating Statute 000-Alpha by casting magic without an intact brainstem—and by 9:00:02 AM, the secondary effect of his next spell (violating the new Statute 000-Beta: Unauthorized Necromantic Self-Resurrection on School Grounds) knitted his neurons back together at the subatomic level before gravity could even pull his eyelids down.

Harry sneezed, blowing a tiny wisp of pink vapor out of his left nostril, and immediately launched into the rest of the legal code.

The second week’s devastation made the first look like a rehearsal. Because the Wizengamot had passed four hundred absurdly specific new laws to trip him up—It is illegal to turn the moon green while hopping on one foot; It is illegal to summon an extinct species of Pleistocene megafauna into the Prime Minister’s bedroom—the planet spent the next twelve hours enduring a surreal nightmare of green lunar tides and woolly mammoths crashing through the floorboards of 10 Downing Street.

When he finished at sunset, he didn't even look at Dumbledore. He just grabbed a plate of sausages and left.

By the third week, despair had curdled into madness.

In the sunken war room beneath Whitehall, the surviving members of the Wizengamot were screaming at each other over piles of parchment. They had tried anti-magic fields. They had tried dropping a tactical nuclear warhead on the castle on Wednesday afternoon—Harry had casually caught the falling warhead with a stasis charm in his sleep because Unlicensed Possession of Fissile Material wasn't due to be broken until Monday morning, and he hated spoilers.

Sitting in the back row of the gallery was Arthur Weasley.

His clothes were rumpled, his red hair sticking up in tufts. For two weeks, while the Unspeakables debated quantum soul-shredding and the Muggle generals argued about orbital kinetic strikes, Arthur had been staring at his own lifework: the Muggle Protection Act. He knew better than anyone in that room how loopholes worked, because he had written dozens of them into his own laws so he could keep a flying Ford Anglia in his shed.

"Stop writing laws," Arthur said.

His voice wasn't loud, but in the lull between Fudge’s sobbing and Amelia Bones’s shouting, it carried across the stone chamber. Every head turned toward him.

"What did you say, Weasley?" Scrimgeour barked.

"You're giving him ammunition," Arthur said, standing up slowly. His hands trembled on the brass railing, but his eyes were fixed on the massive leather-bound tome of the Wizengamot Charter sitting on the central table. "Every law we write, he has to break. The more complex we make them, the worse the collateral damage gets. He’s an engine that runs on our legislation."

Dumbledore leaned forward, a spark of terrible comprehension igniting in his tired eyes. "Arthur... what are you proposing?"

"Dissolve the Ministry," Arthur said simply. "Convene the ICW, call the Muggle parliaments, and sign a global omnibus bill before 9:00 AM tomorrow. Repeal every law. Every statute, every decree, every bylaw, every municipal parking regulation on the planet. Complete, total, formal abolition of all legal codes."

"Anarchy?" Amelia Bones whispered, aghast. "Arthur, without law—"

"Without law, there is no crime," Arthur said, his voice rising. "If nothing is illegal, what happens when he steps onto that dais tomorrow morning?"

It took fourteen hours of gunpoint diplomacy, three blood-oaths, and the combined magical coercion of Dumbledore and the remaining world leaders, but at 8:58 AM on Monday morning, the final signature dried on the Global Abolition of Jurisprudence Act. Every constitution, statute book, and Ministry decree was formally nullified and burned in magical fire.

In the Great Hall, Harry stepped onto the dais. He didn't even bring a snack this time. He looked ready for another twelve-hour shift of tedium.

9:00 AM chimed from the clock tower.

Harry raised his hands. The air around him hummed, preparing to shatter the first statute on the register.

And then... nothing happened.

Harry’s fingers stayed splayed in the air. His brow furrowed. He tilted his head, his eyes darting back and forth as his mind reached out across the magical and administrative weave of the planet, scanning the Ministry archives, the ICW ledgers, the Parliamentary rolls, all the way down to the local village council of Hogsmeade.

Empty. Blank. Null.

"Huh," Harry said out loud.

From the shadows near the staff table, Dumbledore, McGonagall, Snape, and Arthur Weasley watched without breathing.

"There are no laws to be broken now?" Harry muttered, lowering his hands and looking down at his palms as if they had malfunctioned. He closed his eyes, running a deep diagnostic scan across the entire human collective consciousness. "Scanning... yep. They’re all formally repealed. Not a single active prohibition on the planet. You guys even legalized murder, tax evasion, and double-parking in the same bill."

Slowly, a smile spread across Harry Potter’s eleven-year-old face.

It wasn't a cruel smile, nor was it the psychotic grin of a Dark Lord. It was the genuine, blindingly bright smile of a child waking up on Christmas morning to find something he actually didn't expect. For the first time in five hundred and ninety-one million lifetimes, his eyes sparked with vivid, terrifying life.

"Finally," Harry breathed. "Something new."

His gaze snapped up, sweeping over the small group of adults huddled near the wall. His green eyes bypassed Dumbledore entirely, skipped over Snape’s pale face, and locked directly onto the man standing in the back in a patched tweed coat.

Harry didn't need to use Legilimency in the traditional sense; after half a billion iterations, he could read the causal history of a room by the way people held their shoulders and the micro-expressions around their eyes.

"Who was the one that came up with the..." Harry trailed off, his eyes narrowing on Arthur’s trembling chin, the ink stains on his cuffs from drafting the omnibus repeal, the way Amelia Bones was unconsciously angling her body to shield him. "Ah. Yes. You."

Harry pointed a finger straight at Arthur Weasley.

"Arthur, wait—" Dumbledore stepped in front of him, wand raised, though there was no conviction left in his arm. "Harry, he only did what you asked! We stopped you!"

"You didn't kill me, Albus, you just unplugged the jukebox," Harry said cheerfully. He took a single step off the dais, and the space between him and Arthur seemed to fold like wet paper. Before anyone could blink, Harry’s small, calloused hand reached out and gripped Arthur’s wrist.

A sound like a harp string snapping echoed through the foundations of Hogwarts. Deep beneath the floor, a silver, impossibly complex geometric brand flared through the fabric of Harry’s sleeve and burned a matching mark straight into the skin of Arthur’s forearm.

"Congratulations, Mr. Weasley," Harry said, his grin wide and sharp. "You’re the first person in four hundred million runs to actually surprise me. You’ll be looping with me from now on."

Arthur gasped, dropping to his knees as fifty billion years of temporal weight brushed against the edges of his mortal mind. "No—Harry, my family—Molly, the boys—"

"See you in a second," Harry said, and snapped his fingers.

The scarlet steam engine of the Hogwarts Express hissed loudly, venting white clouds onto Platform Nine and Three-Quarters.

In the middle of the bustling crowd, standing right beside a stacked trolley of battered trunks and a screeching owl named Errol, Arthur Weasley screamed.

He collapsed onto the concrete platform, clutching his head, hyperventilating as his eyes darted wildly around the station. The sky was clean. The station roof wasn't melted. Around him, witches and wizards walked past in intact robes, giving him odd, concerned looks.

"Arthur! Heavens, Arthur, what is it?"

Molly Weasley was kneeling beside him, her plump hands gripping his shoulders, her face creased with worry. Beside her stood Percy in his brand-new Prefect badge, Fred and George looking startled, little Ginny clinging to Molly’s skirt, and Ron, eleven years old with a smudge of dirt on his nose.

"The—the pigeons," Arthur choked out, grabbing Molly’s arms with bruising force. "Molly, the laws! Did they repeal the laws?! Where's the bunker?!"

"Bunker? Pigeons?" Molly felt his forehead, looking alarmed. "Arthur, dear, you’ve been working too many late nights in that drafty office. Come on, the train’s about to leave, help Ron with his trunk—"

Arthur froze. He looked down at his left wrist. Beneath the frayed cuff of his tweed jacket, there was no mark—yet he could feel it, humming like an overcharged battery right inside his soul. He remembered the ash of the Sorting Hat. He remembered the sky turning the color of bruised meat. He remembered signing the Abolition Act at 8:58 AM.

And then, weaving casually through the crowd of parents and cats, came a skinny boy with messy black hair, taped-together glasses, and baggy clothes that belonged to someone three times his size.

Harry didn't have a trolley. He had banished Hedwig and his trunk directly to the Gryffindor dormitories from Surrey twenty minutes ago so he wouldn't have to wheel them through King's Cross. He strolled right up to the paralyzed Weasley patriarch, his hands tucked into his pockets.

Arthur scrambled backward on the concrete, knocking over Ron’s trunk, his face white as bone. "You—it’s September first! We went back!"

"Welcome to Loop 591,512,080, Arthur," Harry said pleasantly, ignoring the bewildered stares of Molly and the children. "How was your first transit? The nausea usually fades around loop four thousand."

"Who is this?" Ron asked, squinting at Harry’s forehead where the famous lightning scar was visible beneath his bangs. "Blimey, are you—"

"Quiet, Ron," Arthur whispered, his voice cracking with pure terror as he stared up at the eleven-year-old god standing over him. "Harry... please. I can't do this. Billions of years... my mind won't survive it. Let me go. Please, I'll never touch a lawbook again, just let me go!"

Harry looked down at him, the manic glee from the Great Hall settling back into a quiet, ancient melancholy. He crouched down so he was eye-level with the trembling wizard, speaking softly enough that Molly and the kids only heard the whistle of the train.

"Hey, take a breath, Arthur," Harry murmured, patting the older man’s knee. "Don't worry. You’re tethered to me, not the anchor. Whenever you’ve had enough—whether that’s next week, or in ten thousand years after you’ve finally figured out how airplanes stay up—I can sever your tether and release you from the loop. You’ll go right back to normal, forget the whole thing, and live out your life."

Harry stood back up, offering a small, hollow smile that didn't reach his ancient green eyes.

"Unlike me," Harry added quietly. He tapped his watchless wrist. "Come on. Train’s leaving in two minutes, and I want to show you how to hotwire the Hogwarts Express so it flies to Tahiti."
