Prof. McGonagall lowered the frayed leather brim of the Sorting Hat onto Harry Potter’s messy black hair, and for half a second, the Great Hall waited in polite, expectant silence.

Then the Hat screamed—a high, tearing sound like a thousand silk banners ripping in a hurricane—and detonated into a pillar of roaring violet fire.

Flaming scraps of ancient, sentient leather rained down onto the flagstones. McGonagall stumbled backward, her spectacles cracked and her tartan robes smoking at the hem, clutching her chest as the sheer psychic feedback of half a billion lifetimes echoed out of the ashes. At the Gryffindor table, Hermione Granger half-rose from her bench, her hands flying to her mouth, while Ron Weasley froze with a chicken leg halfway to his teeth.

Harry Potter did not flinch. He sat on the three-legged stool, eleven years old, skinny knees poking out from beneath oversized robes, his eyes a flat, terrifyingly ancient shade of green.

He didn't reach for his holly wand. He simply raised his right forefinger and began at Statute 1, Section A, Paragraph 1 of the International Confederation of Wizards’ Code of Conduct: The International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy (1689).

With a casual flick of his wrist, Harry tore a hole in the atmospheric space above London, New York, Tokyo, Paris, and Cairo, projecting a five-mile-high, crystalline hologram of the Hogwarts Great Hall into the night sky of the Muggle world, complete with booming, translated audio and a scrolling ticker of every hidden magical settlement on Earth.

Before Albus Dumbledore could even draw the Elder Wand from his star-spangled sleeve, Harry moved to Statute 2: Treason Against the Sitting Minister. The enchanted ceiling of the Great Hall ripped open, and Cornelius Fudge plummeted through the open air in his striped pajamas, landing face-first in a tureen of leek and potato soup at the Hufflepuff table—bound in chains of solid, transfigured plutonium (Statute 3: Unlawful Creation of Unstable Muggle Elements in a Populated Zone).

"Potter!" Snape surged to his feet at the staff table, his wand whipping up with lethal speed. "Expelli—"

Harry was already on the Decree for the Reasonable Restriction of Underage Sorcery, combined simultaneously with the 1707 Ban on Experimental Breeding, the 1875 Goblin Currency Forgery Act, and the 1717 Classification of Unforgivable Curses.

Snape’s wand turned into a live, screaming Manticore-Flobberworm hybrid that spat a shower of counterfeit, hyper-inflated Galleons into the Ravenclaw benches. Simultaneously, three hundred miles away in Little Hangleton, the wraith of Lord Voldemort was yanked out of the forests of Albania, slammed into a newly conjured, anatomically absurd body made of pure Yorkshire pudding, placed under the Imperius Curse to tap-dance the Macarena, hit with the Cruciatus until the pastry wept, and promptly struck dead with a localized, pinpoint Avada Kedavra—all while Harry’s left pinky twitched to illegally import seventeen thousand flying carpets into downtown Manchester.

He did not stand up from the three-legged stool. He did not shift his posture.

When the Auror Office breached the Great Hall doors twenty minutes later, Harry was inside the Department of Mysteries Sub-Clauses (1792–1844). Dawlish and Kingsley Shacklebolt raised their wands to cast a joint Stunning Spell, only to watch their curses hit an invisible, rotating sphere of illegally modified Time-Turners orbiting Harry’s head. The spells aged into harmless dust before touching his fringe. Without blinking, Harry legally married the Giant Squid to the Prime Minister of Great Britain without a license (Violation 4,012), turned the entire stone facade of Gringotts Wizarding Bank into non-dairy cheese (Violation 4,013), and ripped the Fidelius Charm off every secret on the planet (Violation 4,014).

By midnight, the Great Hall was a graveyard of broken wands andcatatonic wizards.

By four in the morning, he had reached the bottom of the legislative barrel: the 1991 Municipal By-Laws of Hogsmeade Regarding Cauldron-Bottom Thickness and Post-Midnight Owl Hooting. A deafening, supersonic hoot echoed across the Scottish Highlands as millions of thin-bottomed pewter cauldrons rained into the Black Lake.

And because the International Taskforce of Hit-Wizards had just Apparated onto the grounds to fire an orbital bombardment of Fiendfyre at the castle, Harry was not done.

He reversed the list. Bottom to top.

He started with the owl hooting and worked his way back up through fraud, illegal animagus registration, necromancy, blood-magic, elemental devastation, dark artifact proliferation, and mass-scale memory modification, until he reached Statute 1 once more at precisely 6:14 PM the following evening, broadcasting the smoldering, apocalyptic legal ruin of both societies in high-definition across the surface of the Moon.

The silence that followed was heavy enough to crush bone.

Across the globe, the Muggle stock markets had ceased to exist. The United Nations was in an emergency session trying to process why the Eiffel Tower was currently braided like a challah loaf and why dragons were nesting on the Pentagon. The Ministry of Magic’s Atrium was full of three feet of sentient, unlicensed turnip-sludge.

Inside the ruined Great Hall, students were huddled under the tables, weeping or staring into the middle distance. Snape was wedged head-first into the Slytherin hourglass, covered in counterfeit gold and pudding residue. McGonagall was sitting on the floor, her hands trembling violently as she stared at the boy on the stool.

At the high table, Albus Dumbledore stood alone. The Elder Wand hung limply from his fingers. His half-moon spectacles were gone, his silver beard was singed black on the left side, and for the first time in a century, the greatest sorcerer in the world looked every single day of his age. He looked terrified.

Harry finally lowered his hands to his lap. He exhaled a slow, quiet breath, turned his head toward the High Table, pointed one small finger at Dumbledore, and gave a lazy, two-fingered wave. Come here.

Dumbledore’s boots crunched over shattered glass and petrified cauldron fragments. He stopped at the base of the dais, looking down at the eleven-year-old boy.

"Harry," Dumbledore whispered, his voice cracking like dry parchment. "My boy... what... why?"

"I needed you to listen," Harry said. His voice was still the high, clear soprano of a child, which made the dead, mechanical cadence of his words infinitely worse. "In Loop forty-thousand, I tried showing you my memories in the Pensieve. You thought Voldemort had possessed me and locked me in Nurmengard for your own 'greater good.' In Loop twelve-million, I killed Voldemort on the train and handed you the Horcruxes in a basket. You spent the next seven years trying to 'rehabilitate' my soul because I knew too much Dark magic. If I'm polite, you manipulate me. If I'm a prodigy, you study me. If I'm a Dark Lord, you fight me."

Harry gestured vaguely at the hole in the roof, through which the glowing, holographic face of Cornelius Fudge was still visible on the surface of the waxing moon.

"I calculated it around Loop four-hundred-million," Harry continued conversationally. "The only way to get every witch, wizard, goblin, centaur, and Muggle on Earth to stop talking, stop plotting, and accept my premise within twenty-four hours was to commit every codified crime in human and magical history, in alphabetical and chronological order, twice, without leaving this stool."

Hermione Granger peeked out from beneath the Gryffindor table, her eyes wide, bloodless, and fixated on him. "L-Loop?" she squeaked. "You... you're in a temporal recursion?"

"Five hundred and ninety-one million, five hundred and twelve thousand, and seventy-nine," Harry said, glancing at her. "Hello, Hermione. You usually figure it out around third year if I drop hints, and then you spend forty years trying to build an arithmantic anchor that accidentally turns the sun into a lemon. Don't bother."

Dumbledore’s knees actually buckled; he caught himself on the edge of the staff table. "Five hundred and ninety-one... million? Harry, the human mind cannot—"

"It can't," Harry agreed pleasantly. "Mine broke somewhere around the eighty-million mark. Then it put itself back together wrong. Then it got efficient. And now?" He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees, looking into Dumbledore’s pale blue eyes. "I’m bored, Albus."

Nobody breathed.

"I’ve saved the world every way it can be saved," Harry said, ticking points off on his fingers. "I’ve burned it down every way it can be burned. I’ve married everyone in this room at least once—sorry about that, Hagrid, it was a weird century—I’ve conquered the galaxy, I’ve lived as a badger in Wales for sixty years, and I’ve memorized the atomic trajectory of every raindrop that falls on Scotland between 1991 and 2084. Nothing kills the loop. If I die of old age, I wake up in the cupboard. If I step in front of a Killing Curse, I wake up on the train. If I blow up the planet, I wake up right as McGonagall calls my name."

Harry hopped off the stool. His shoes clicked neatly against the stone.

"So here is the deal," Harry announced to the hall—and, via the lingering atmospheric amplification charms, to the seven billion people listening in horrified silence across the globe. "Next Sunday at noon, I am going to sit down wherever I happen to be, and I am going to break every law on the books again. Top to bottom, bottom to top. Including any new ones you pass between now and then. And I’m going to do it every single week until someone in this world figures out a way to kill me permanently so I don't wake up on September first again."

He walked down the steps of the dais, stepping over a softly whimpering Quirrell (whose turban had been legally evicted under the 1642 Parasitic Squatting Act six hours ago). Stopping by the Gryffindor table, Harry picked up a platter of untouched roast beef sandwiches and tucked a bottle of pumpkin juice under his arm.

"You have seven days," Harry said, pausing by the great oak doors. "Pool your resources. Hire the Unspeakables, call the Muggle nuclear physicists, dig up Salazar Slytherin's bones, I don't care. Good luck."

He pushed the door open with his shoulder and strolled out toward the Forbidden Forest, chewing on a sandwich.

The first week was the Week of Legislative Hubris.

The International Confederation of Wizards, in an unprecedented joint session with the United Nations Security Council, decided that if Harry Potter was bound by some psychotic, self-imposed compulsion to break every law in order, they would simply weaponize the legal code.

"It's a muggle logic bomb!" Hermione Granger argued fiercely on the third day. Despite being eleven, she had been drafted directly into the Department of Mysteries' crisis room because she was the only person in the castle Harry had spoken to like an equal, and frankly, the adult wizards were out of ideas. She stood on a chair in front of a massive blackboard covered in legislative flowcharts. "He said he breaks them in perfect order. If we pass fifty-million redundant, hyper-specific laws—like The Prohibition of Balancing Three Teacups on a Kneazle's Nose in Aberdeen—it will act as a buffer! While he's busy executing the harmless laws, our strike teams can hit him with the experimental soul-obliteration array!"

"Brilliant," muttered Kingsley Shacklebolt, rubbing his bloodshot eyes. Next to him, Severus Snape was furiously brewing a vat of weaponized Draught of Living Death mixed with Muggle nerve agents provided by MI6, while Dumbledore pored over ancient Sumerian scrolls with Nicolas Flamel on a two-way mirror.

By Saturday midnight, the Wizengamot and the ICW had passed 4,210,889 new laws. They ranged from the Mandatory Wearing of Lime-Green Socks on Tuesdays Act to the Absolute Prohibition of Harry James Potter Breathing Oxygen Within the British Isles.

They thought that last one was particularly clever. If he had to break the law, wait—no, if breathing was illegal, he'd have to breathe, which meant—actually, the Unspeakables got into a fistfight over the semantics five minutes before noon on Sunday.

At 12:00:00 PM on Sunday, Harry Potter was sitting on a mossy boulder at the edge of the Great Lake, skipping stones.

At 12:00:01 PM, every single person in the United Kingdom was forcibly dressed in lime-green socks EXCEPT Harry, four million Kneazles materialized in Aberdeen with teacups glued to their snouts, the oxygen in the British Isles was briefly transfigured into breathable nitrous oxide so Harry wasn't technically breathing oxygen while still breaking the Atmospheric Tampering Act of 1991, and the Department of Mysteries' experimental soul-obliteration array was illegally sold on the black market to a syndicate of Peruvian vampires before it could finish charging.

He cleared all four million new laws, plus the original code, in nineteen minutes flat. Because he had practiced speed-casting for three hundred million years, adding more laws didn't slow him down; it just increased the collateral chaos per second. By 12:20 PM, the Moon was plaid, the Atlantic Ocean was mildly carbonated, and Cornelius Fudge was back in the soup tureen.

"Nice try with the Kneazles, Hermione!" Harry’s voice boomed across the skies of Earth before he went back to eating a treacle tart by the lake. "See you next Sunday!"

By Week Four, the world was unrecognizable.

Muggle and magical borders had completely dissolved out of sheer shared trauma. A joint headquarters had been established in the ruins of Geneva, where Vatican exorcists, Unspeakables, Russian thermonuclear engineers, and Goblin curse-breakers shared coffee out of styrofoam cups and argued over quantum mechanics.

Nothing worked.

In Week Two, they dropped a tactical thermonuclear warhead wrapped in a Fidelius Charm directly onto his head while he was napping in the Owlery. Harry didn't even wake up; a passive contingency ward he'd woven into his own skin during Loop 104,000,000 absorbed the kinetic and thermal energy and used it to illegally heat every swimming pool in Sweden past the municipal safety limit.

In Week Three, Dumbledore, Flamel, and a temporarily resurrected Grindelwald (pardoned specifically for the occasion) attempted to trap Harry inside a pocket dimension devoid of time. Harry broke out in four seconds because Unlicensed Dimensional Excavation was #884 on the Tuesday morning municipal annex, and he needed to be in Scotland to violate a zoning ordinance.

It was on the Thursday of Week Five that Ronald Weasley, of all people, broke the stalemate.

Ron was sitting in the corner of the Hogwarts Library—which now served as the forward command post for the Allied Survival Coalition—playing wizard’s chess against himself. Around him, fifty exhausted geniuses were screaming at each other over chalkboards.

"If we pass a law making it illegal for him to kill himself—" an Unspeakable shouted.

"Then he breaks it by killing himself, which resets the loop, you imbecile!" Snape snarled, his hair hanging in lank, greasy curtains over a face hollow from four weeks of zero sleep. "He told us! If he dies normally, time resets and we all go back to being oblivious sheep while he tortures us again! We have to destroy his temporal anchor before his heart stops!"

"We can't even get near him on Sundays because the legal backlash forms a defensive maelstrom!" McGonagall cried, slamming her hands on a table. "The more laws we pass to restrict him, the more spells he casts at noon! Last week, someone in the French Ministry made it illegal to turn the River Seine into mayonnaise, and now Paris smells like eggs!"

Ron frowned at his chessboard. His white knight was trapped between two black rooks. Every move on the board was a loss.

"Wait a minute," Ron said aloud.

Nobody listened.

"Oi," Ron said louder, standing up and kicking his chair back. Hermione looked up from a stack of Arithmancy tables, her hair bushier than athornbush. "You lot are all supposed to be dead clever, right? Why do we keep giving him ammunition?"

"We aren't giving him ammunition, Mr. Weasley," Dumbledore said tiredly from his armchair by the fire. "We are attempting to construct a statutory cage—"

"No, you're giving him a checklist!" Ron pointed a smudged finger at the giant scroll of the International Wizarding Code pinned to the wall. "He's mental, right? Completely barmy. Five hundred million years of the same day has turned his brains into scrambled eggs. He's got a routine. Like Percy when he lines up his quills. He only goes off on Sundays, and he only casts spells that break a law. During the week, all he does is wander around eating sandwiches and petting Fang!"

Snape stared at the eleven-year-old redhead. Slowly, the Potions Master’s dark eyes narrowed.

Hermione’s quill dropped from her hand, splattering black ink across her shoes. "Oh," she breathed.

Dumbledore sat up straight. "Severus. Miss Granger. What are you thinking?"

"A Boggart," Hermione whispered, her mind racing at terrifying speed. "Or a House-Elf's compulsion! When a mind endures half a billion cycles of sensory deprivation—because that's what a loop is, philosophical sensory deprivation—it latches onto rigid, algorithmic rules to keep from dissolving entirely! He didn't choose to break every law just to scare us. The algorithm is the only thing keeping his consciousness moving forward!"

"And if you take away the pieces on the board..." Ron said slowly.

"...there are no legal moves left to make," Snape finished, a cold, fierce light dawning in his eyes for the first time in a month. "If there are no laws..."

"Then there are no crimes," Dumbledore whispered.

The Great Repeal of 1991 took forty-eight hours of absolute, screaming pandemonium across every parliament, senate, diet, and ministry on Earth.

It was the hardest thing humanity had ever done. Convincing politicians, monarchs, and Ministry bureaucrats to voluntarily abolish their own authority was harder than fighting Voldemort, Grindelwald, and a nuclear winter combined.

"You want us to repeal the Statute of Secrecy?!" Cornelius Fudge shrieked in the Wizengamot chamber on Friday afternoon, still wearing a bathrobe after the soup incident. "And the ban on the Unforgivables?! Madness! Anarchy! Murder will be legal!"

"Murder is ALREADY happening every Sunday at twelve-oh-four when Potter gets to the Homicide Act of 1532, you blistering idiot!" Amelia Bones roared, physically grabbing Fudge by the lapels and shaking him until his teeth rattled. "He resurrects the victims at twelve-nineteen just so he can violate the Necromancy Prohibition of 1604, and frankly, my niece says dying once a week is ruining her OWL preparation! SIGN THE PARCHMENT!"

By Saturday night, the bonfires of legislation burned in every capital city.

The British Parliament dissolved the Common Law and all statutory acts back to the Magna Carta. The United States Congress suspended the Constitution and every federal, state, and municipal code. The International Confederation of Wizards burned the Book of Statutes in the center of Geneva. The Goblins annulled the Treaty of 1612. Even Hogwarts repealed the school rules—Filch had to be stunned and locked in a broom cupboard by the twins when he tried to defend Rule #342 (No Dungbombs in the Corridors).

Every parking regulation. Every tax code. Every ban on dark magic, jaywalking, treason, cauldron thickness, and murder. Gone. Earth became, on paper and in magic, a sphere of absolute, total, uncodified anarchy.

Sunday morning dawned crisp, clear, and dead silent.

At 11:55 AM, Harry Potter walked out of the castle courtyard and sat down on the mossy boulder by the Great Lake. He set a plate of jam tarts beside him.

Three hundred yards away, standing on the stone viaduct, Dumbledore, McGonagall, Snape, Hermione, and Ron watched through omnioculars. Behind them stood a silent army of Aurors, Unspeakables, and Muggle scientists, holding no weapons at all.

11:59 AM.

Harry dusted the crumbs off his palms. He didn't look angry or theatrical; he just looked profoundly, exhaustingly tired. Like a factory worker clocking in for his five-hundred-millionth shift.

12:00:00 PM.

Harry raised his right forefinger to break Statute 1, Section A, Paragraph 1 of the International Code.

He stopped.

His finger hovered in the cool Scottish air. A tiny furrow appeared between his eyebrows, right below the faded lightning-bolt scar.

Through the omnioculars, Hermione gasped. "Look at his eyes."

The flat, mechanical green was gone. For the first time since the Sorting Hat had burst into flames, Harry’s pupils dilated. He reached out with his magic—that vast, terrifying, half-billion-year-old ocean of awareness that usually read the rigid lattice of human and magical law across the planet like a sheet of music.

There was no sheet music.

He shifted his finger slightly, trying to trigger Statute 2. Nothing. There was no Minister of Magic; the office had been abolished at 4:00 AM. There was no treason. He tried to drop down to the Underage Sorcery Decree. Nothing. Anyone of any age could cast anything. He searched for the Cauldron Thickness Act. Gone. He searched for a speeding limit in Ohio. Gone.

"What..." Harry’s voice was small. It didn't boom from the clouds. It barely carried across the pebbles of the lakeshore. "What did you do?"

Dumbledore lowered his omnioculars and walked alone down the grassy slope toward the lake. He kept his hands in full view, empty, the Elder Wand left behind on McGonagall’s desk.

"We listened to you, Harry," Dumbledore said gently, stopping ten feet from the boulder. "You told us to get creative."

Harry stared at his own hand. It was trembling. The terrifying, godlike titan who had folded the Eiffel Tower and killed Voldemort with a pastry looked down at his eleven-year-old fingers, and his breathing began to hitch.

"There's nothing to break," Harry whispered. "I checked the municipal codes in Kuala Lumpur. I checked the Goblin blood-oaths. You... you erased all of it. Why would you erase all of it? Now there's no order. I have to go in order. Top to bottom, bottom to top, or the week doesn't count, if the week doesn't count I don't know which loop it is—"

"It is Loop five hundred and ninety-one million, five hundred and twelve thousand, and seventy-nine," Hermione called out, walking down the hill behind Dumbledore, with Ron right at her elbow. She stopped beside the Headmaster, looking at the boy on the rock not like a monster, but like a puzzle that had finally shown its seams. "And you don't know what happens next, do you?"

Harry froze.

Slowly, he raised his head.

"In half a billion loops," Hermione said, her voice shaking only a little, "has the entire world ever abolished every single law on Earth just to stop you from having a routine?"

Harry’s mouth opened. Closed.

He searched his vast, infinite, suffocating memory. Five hundred and ninety-one million lifetimes. Wars, plagues, alliances, traps, prisons, apocalypses. Never once had seven billion people collectively agreed to throw away civilization's rulebook just to leave him standing in an empty room with nothing to push against.

"No," Harry breathed.

And as the word left his lips, a strange, golden crackle of energy sparked across the surface of the Black Lake.

Up on the viaduct, the Unspeakables' temporal monitors—which had been flatlining in a closed causal loop since September first—suddenly shrieked to life. Needles redlined. Crystals shattered.

"The anchor!" Snape’s voice echoed from the bridge. "Albus! The paradox wasn't magical—it was causal! The loop only resets when he reaches a state of total predictability! He trapped himself!"

Down by the water, Harry looked at his hands again. The grey, timeless pallor that had hung over him like a shroud was evaporating in the autumn sunlight. A gust of wind blew across the lake, ruffling his messy black hair, and Harry flinched as if the cold air had actually bitten his skin for the first time in eons.

"I didn't know this would happen," Harry whispered, a tear finally spilling over his lower lash, cutting through the ancient dust on his cheek. "I didn't... I didn't know what you'd do."

"Neither do we, Harry," Dumbledore said softly, sitting down on the grass beside the boulder, completely ignoring the dampness soaking into his robes. He reached over and picked up one of the jam tarts from Harry’s plate. "And frankly, tomorrow morning, when Cornelius realizes he is no longer legally protected from being turned into a ferret by his constituents, things are going to get extraordinarily messy. We are going to have a very busy week putting the world back together."

Ron ambled over and kicked a flat pebble into the water. "Yeah, well. Can we leave the cauldron-thickness law out of it? Percy hasn't shut up about it since Tuesday, and I'd quite like it if he had nothing to write to the Ministry about."

Harry stared at Ron. Then at Hermione. Then at Dumbledore, who was calmly eating a jam tart in the middle of global anarchy.

A strange, rusty sound came out of Harry Potter’s throat. It took him three tries to realize he was laughing. He slumped back against the mossy stone, looking up at a sky that was completely empty of holograms, waiting for a Monday he hadn't already seen half a billion times.
