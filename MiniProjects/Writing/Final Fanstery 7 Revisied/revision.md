The Nibelheim Revision

The basement of the Shinra Manor smelled of rotting vellum, formaldehyde, and ancient, stagnant dust. For three days and three nights, Sephiroth had immersed himself in the mad, scratching scrawls of Professor Gast and the redacted, cold files of the Shinra Electric Power Company.

He found the truth. He found Jenova. He found the "Ancient" that wasn't an Ancient at all, but a stellar parasite preserved in a cryogenic womb of industrial steel.

According to the script written by his own fragile DNA, this was the exact moment Sephiroth was supposed to lose his mind. The poetry of madness demanded he set Nibelheim ablaze, slaughter the innocent, and march toward the Mt. Nibel Mako Reactor to reclaim his "mother" and purge the planet of the human infection.

Instead, Sephiroth leaned back in his heavy oak chair, crossed his long, leather-clad legs, and let out a soft, profoundly weary sigh.

"Pilot the planet," he murmured to the empty, shadowy room. "And then what?"

He closed his eyes, imagining the logistics. Suppose he did merge with the Lifestream. Suppose he successfully commandeered this massive, floating rock and sailed it through the dark cosmos like a celestial galleon. Where was he going? What was the destination? More importantly, who was going to handle the navigation? The idea of spending eternity as a disembodied cosmic entity, steering a ball of dirt through a vacuum with nothing but the screaming souls of the deceased for company, sounded extraordinarily tedious.

Furthermore, he began to look at his hands—hands that had slain thousands in the name of a corporate logo. He thought about his salary. He thought about his benefits package. He thought about the fact that he hadn't taken a proper vacation since the Wutai War began.

What am I actually getting out of this deal? he wondered.
The Sudden Pursuit of Leisure

When Sephiroth emerged from the manor, he didn't have his Masamune drawn. Instead, he walked into the Nibelheim general store, ignored the terrifyingly tense townspeople, and began browsing the small, dusty bookshelf in the corner.

He bypassed the tactical manuals and military histories. His fingers lingered over a botanical field guide, a poorly translated volume on ancient Chocobo husbandry, and a thick treatise titled The Wealth of Nations: A Pre-Mako Analysis of Macroeconomics.

He bought them all, charged them to the Shinra corporate card, and went to sit by the town square's water tower.

For a week, Cloud Strife and Zack Fair stood guard outside his door, sweating through their uniforms, terrified that the legendary First Class SOLDIER was suffering a quiet, catastrophic psychotic break. Every now and then, Zack would peek through the window, only to see Sephiroth meticulously pressing local ferns between the pages of a heavy ledger, or furiously scratching out algebraic equations on a legal pad.

By week two, Sephiroth had completed his reading. And by week three, he had reached a staggering, unavoidable conclusion.

Shinra was run by absolute idiots.
The Macroeconomic Apocalypse

It wasn't the morality of Mako extraction that bothered Sephiroth; it was the sheer, staggering, offensive mathematical stupidity of it.

He returned to Midgar not as a conqueror, but as an auditor. He bypassed the SOLDIER floor entirely and marched straight into the corporate archives, demanding a century's worth of geological surveys and energy consumption projections.

The data was damning.

He sat down with a calculator, a pot of tea, and a mounting sense of intellectual disgust. Mako was the literal lifeblood of the planet. Sucking it out caused the surrounding land to wither, turn to ash, and die. At the current rate of extraction, the planet would be an uninhabitable, desiccated husk within eighty to one hundred years.

"Why?" Sephiroth muttered to himself, staring at a pie chart. "If the planet dies, the market dies. If the market dies, there are no consumers. If there are no consumers, who is buying the electricity?"

He realized, with a chill that had nothing to do with Jenova’s alien cells, that Shinra wasn’t actually running a sustainable business. They were running a multi-billion-gil suicide pact. They were squeezing the orange until it burst, completely oblivious to the fact that they were standing inside the orange.

If they invested in geothermal energy, wind-turbine arrays in the Corel region, or even harnessed the kinetic output of the massive oceanic currents near Costa del Sol, the energy yield might be lower in the short term, but the total cumulative output over an infinite timeline would possess a massive, unquantifiable gap compared to Mako.

"They aren't capitalists," Sephiroth whispered, horrified. "They're just... clumsy."
The Boardroom Shockwave

President Shinra was in the middle of an executive briefing on the upper plate when the heavy oak doors of the boardroom didn't just open—they were neatly, precisely unlatched by a man holding a massive leather briefcase.

Sephiroth stepped inside. He wasn't wearing his pauldrons. He was wearing a impeccably tailored, three-piece charcoal suit that he had forced a Shinra tailor to make over a weekend. Behind him walked a small team of terrified, middle-tier corporate lawyers he had aggressively poached from the Department of Administrative Research.

He slammed a three-thousand-page document onto the mahogany table.

"What is the meaning of this, Sephiroth?" President Shinra barked, puffing on his cigar. "You have duties in the field. The remnants of Wutai—"

"Wutai is a rounding error, Mr. President," Sephiroth said, his voice smooth, terrifyingly calm, and entirely devoid of its usual military cadence. "We have far more pressing matters. Specifically, intellectual property theft, corporate mismanagement, and a catastrophic breach of fiduciary duty to the shareholders—of which, as of nine o'clock this morning, I am the majority stakeholder."

The room went dead silent. Palmer dropped his lard-laced tea. Reeve blinked rapidly behind his spectacles. Heidegger’s boisterous laugh died a sudden, choked death in his throat.

"I have spent the last month reviewing my origins," Sephiroth continued, pacing the length of the boardroom. "I am not a human being. I am the direct biological product of Project Jenova. Jenova is an extraterrestrial organism that arrived via a meteor collision approximately two thousand years ago. By all legal definitions recognized under Midgar Corporate Law, Article 4, Section 2 regarding 'Extraterrestrial and Anomalous Natural Resources,' Jenova is considered an unclaimed sovereign entity."

He paused, leaning over the President’s desk, his glowing green Mako eyes burning with a terrifyingly bureaucratic intensity.

"As her sole surviving, direct genetic heir—and possessing over ninety-eight percent of her genomic sequence—I have successfully filed for retroactive copyright and patent ownership over the Jenova cell line. This includes, but is not limited to: the SOLDIER program, the physical enhancements of all First, Second, and Third-class personnel, the biological blueprints of all bio-weapons currently engineered by Professor Hojo, and the branding rights to the name 'Sephiroth' which you have used to sell action figures without my express written consent."

From the back corner, Professor Hojo, who had been sneering in his usual manic fashion, froze. His glasses slid down his nose.

    Hojo: "What?"

    President Shinra: "What?"

"Furthermore," Sephiroth said, snapping his fingers. A lawyer trembling so violently he could barely hold his briefcase stepped forward and handed a cease-and-desist order to Heidegger. "Because my genetic material was utilized without the consent of the donor—or her estate—to stabilize and empower the military arm of this company, I am suing the Shinra Electric Power Company for back-pay, accrued interest, and punitive damages totaling four hundred and fifty billion gil. Or, alternatively, I will accept full executive control of the energy division."

"You're insane," Heidegger sputtered, his face turning an impressive shade of crimson. "You're a weapon! We built you!"

"You cultivated me," Sephiroth corrected gently, tapping a manicured fingernail against the table. "Much like a farmer cultivates a rare hybrid crop. But the farmer does not own the concept of photosynthesis, Heidegger. Do you want to do this the hard way, or the profitable way? Because if we go to court, I will personally dissolve the SOLDIER program by invoking my right to recall my own cellular property. Do you want to see what happens to your stock price when every First Class asset suddenly walks out the door to form a private consultancy firm?"
The Aftermath

Down in the training rooms, Zack Fair sat on a bench, his head buried in his hands, staring at a newly printed corporate memo.

    MEMO TO ALL SOLDIER PERSONNEL:

    Effective immediately, all combat operations are suspended pending a structural audit of the Mako Energy Sector. Please report to the newly formed 'Department of Sustainability and Genetic Licensing' for your new corporate lanyards. Casual Fridays are now mandatory.

    — Sephiroth, Chief Executive Officer of Planetary Assets

Cloud Strife stood next to him, looking entirely bewildered. "Zack? What does this mean? Are we... are we not fighting monsters anymore?"

Zack grimaced, a deep, painful expression of sheer, unadulterated cosmic confusion. "Cloud... I think... I think Sephiroth just bought the company. I think we have to do annual performance reviews now."

Meanwhile, up on the executive level, Rufus Shinra stood by the floor-to-ceiling windows, watching the Mako reactors slowly spin down, their green glows dimming as geothermal blueprints were rolled out across the lower sectors.

Rufus took a slow sip of his scotch, looking at the headline of the Midgar Evening Gazette, which featured a picture of Sephiroth wearing a sharp silk tie, holding a small potted fern, and smiling politely for the cameras.

Rufus began to laugh. It started as a chuckle, then built into a deep, echoing, genuine belly laugh that filled the pristine corporate hallway.

"The calamity that descended from the skies," Rufus gasped, wiping a tear of pure amusement from his eye. "The ultimate lifeform... and he’s defeating my father with corporate restructuring and aggressive tax loopholes. Beautiful. Absolutely beautiful."
