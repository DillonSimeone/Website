Universal Music Design

As a member and Lead Design Engineer for Universal Music Design, I worked at the intersection of acoustic innovation, assistive music technology, and sensory accessibility. One of our landmark live performances was "The Process is the Solution" (#deafjazz)—an experimental concert blending acoustic performance, electronic synthesis, and tactile vibrations.

During the performance, I played the Moog Theremini, using continuous gesture and non-contact spatial control to craft ethereal, expressive electronic textures. Time and again, listeners and audience members have shared how intriguing, unexpected, and resonant the music was.

Performance: "The Process is the Solution" (#deafjazz)

In addition to stage performance and tactile instrument design, I engineered the Universal Music Design web platform. Using the Web Audio API to perform real-time FFT frequency analysis on streaming audio, I built a reactive fog simulator that translates audio frequencies and volume into color shifts and atmospheric density. High notes generate a radiant purple haze while deep bass pulses crimson, letting Deaf and Hard of Hearing visitors experience sound as a visual translation.

Note: The original domain has expired, but the website can still be visited at the live deployment (bejewelled-kelpie-6a1941.netlify.app) or explored via the local engineering log (UMD Dev Log & Session Utility).

Confluence, one of the first recording studios founded by a Deaf music producer, captured our shared ethos: “We take pride in pushing boundaries, creating an environment where innovation thrives, and where sound is not just heard but felt.”
