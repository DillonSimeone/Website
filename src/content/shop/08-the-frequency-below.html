A Deaf person raised to pass. A nation tired of almost. And an island, finally, that does not ask them to be anything else.

The Frequency Below is an 86-page, 20-chapter alternate-history novella exploring Deaf cultural sovereignty, institutional erasure, and the physical reality of sound, told across three acts and dual perspectives.

Synopsis: In an alternate mid-century America with no statutory disability protections, Eli navigates a hearing corporate office in a state of continuous, exhausting performance. Raised oral and trained through thousands of hours of speech drills to pass, he reconstructs sixty percent of every meeting from lip movements and shoulder nods, worn down by colleagues who whisper critical updates rather than taking five seconds to write a note. When he reads about the nascent "Deaf Territories" in the evening paper, he feels nothing.

In parallel, Rowan, a Deaf percussive composer, experiences sound not through the air, but through bone conduction, contact microphones, and thirty-hertz sub oscillators bolted to steel beams. Discovered by a Manhattan record scout, Rowan is quickly packaged by the commercial music machine as a "fascinatingly primitive" curiosity, his technical mastery erased by critics, his signing smoothed into tragedy by label-hired interpreters who speak before his hands have finished.

When Rowan abandons the hearing concert halls for the Vineyard, his search for sovereign sound collides with a national strike, a state department quarantine, and an island that has built a society on kinetic vibration and fluent visual sign.
