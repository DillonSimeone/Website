A Deaf person raised to pass. A nation tired of almost. And an island, finally, that does not ask them to be anything else.

I wrote this alternate-history novella about what happens when Deaf people stop apologizing for existing and build their own world.

Growing up Deaf, you spend a lifetime watching hearing culture treat sound like it only belongs to the ear. But in physics and in our bodies, sound is mechanical vibration, fluid pressure, and bone conduction: something you feel in your ribs, your fingertips, and the floorboards beneath your feet.

The story follows two characters in a mid-century America with zero disability protections:

Eli is an office worker worn down by decades of speech drills, lip-reading fatigue, and the exhausting daily performance of trying to "pass" in a hearing corporate world that can't be bothered to write a note.

Rowan is a Deaf percussive musician who doesn't care about hearing music. He bolts sub-bass transducers to wood floors, hooks contact mics to steel beams, and builds a sovereign sound through raw kinetic impact.

When an autonomous Deaf community takes root on Martha's Vineyard, where sign language is the universal tongue of fishermen and town hall alike, their lives collide over a simple question: what does it look like to stop translating yourself for a world that refuses to listen?
