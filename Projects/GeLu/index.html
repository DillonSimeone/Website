Vibration-Matched Illumination

One of the hardest challenges in haptic engineering is demonstrating physical tactile vibrations on video. If you record a bracer vibrating against someone's wrist, the camera sees virtually nothing.

To solve this, I designed GeLu's addressable RGB lighting engine to synchronize precisely with the incoming frequency harmonics and motor attack envelope. When heavy sub-bass hits, the LEDs pulse in deep hues matching the tactile surge. When higher acoustic transients arrive, the LEDs flash in rapid harmony.

This design breakthrough allowed both Deaf performers and sighted audiences to share the same intuitive sensory experience, directly catalyzing my first peer-reviewed academic publication at NIME 2024.

Transient Kick Detection Logic

The firmware compares the instantaneous audio band energy against a smoothed exponential moving average. When a sharp attack occurs (such as a snare strike or bass drop where energy / smoothEnergy > transientThreshold), the system injects an immediate transientBoostDuty burst for 150ms before returning to normal power-curve tracking. This prevents haptic lag and gives percussive beats crisp physical punch.

The Evolution of GeLu

From conceptual research at CymaSpace under Universal Music Design to independent production, custom PCBs, and wearable field deployments.

GELU 1: Felt Sound Research

Initial wearable exploration pairing wireless ESP32-S3 microcontrollers with embedded TensorFlow Lite gesture machine learning models and preliminary vibrotactile nodes.

GELU 2: Rapid Prototyping

Iterative custom PCBs, integrated thumbpad capacitive triggers, tactile motor drivers, and Bambu Lab ergonomic wrist enclosures for live musical performances.

GELU 3: Research Grade

Commercial-ready refinement featuring the 16kHz I2S INMP441 FFT engine, vibration-matched RGB illumination, and standalone captive portal configuration.
