Run Speech Bridge Locally on Windows

The standalone Rust binary (speech-bridge.exe) is only ~630 KB because it dynamically loads its neural speech models and portable CUDA libraries from local subfolders. Instead of making you install Python, Conda, or the NVIDIA CUDA Toolkit by hand, we bundled a one-click installer (Setup.bat) so going from download to live captions takes just three clicks:

CLICK 01 · GET THE FOLDER

Download Speech Bridge

Grab the speech-bridge folder containing speech-bridge.exe, Setup.bat, setup.ps1, and your customizable hotwords.txt glossary (Windows 10/11 64-bit; NVIDIA GPU recommended, CPU fallback supported).

CLICK 02 · RUN SETUP.BAT

Auto-Fetch Models & GPU DLLs

Double-click Setup.bat. It automatically downloads and verifies the portable sherpa-onnx CUDA runtime, NVIDIA CUDA 12 / cuDNN 9 DLLs, neural models, and llama.cpp into local folders, then creates a Desktop shortcut.

CLICK 03 · LAUNCH & SPEAK

Open speech-bridge.exe

Setup.bat launches speech-bridge.exe automatically once downloads finish (or double-click the new Desktop shortcut anytime). Pin it to your taskbar, verify RX: ARMED is green, and start captioning 100% offline.

// 03 :: SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

How Sound Becomes Accurate Captions in 7 Stages

Instead of dumping raw laptop mic audio into a single lightweight CPU model, Speech Bridge processes every 32-millisecond slice of sound through a zero-allocation Rust pipeline that uses both your CPU and GPU FP16 Tensor Cores for what each does best.

STAGE 01 · CAPTURE

Multi-Mic Active-SNR Fusion

Captures 1 mic or 2+ mics simultaneously via Windows WASAPI. Every 32 ms it scores each mic's SNR and crossfades seamlessly to whichever mic is closest to the active speaker.

0 heap allocs · [i16; 512]
STAGE 02 · ECHO GUARD

Automatic TTS Echo Gate & Word Tracking

When you type a reply and press Enter, Piper TTS speaks aloud while bolding and underlining each spoken word in real time, and an atomic gate mutes incoming mic frames (+320 ms reverb tail) so your own voice never echoes into HEARD.

Lock-free AtomicBool
STAGE 03 · CLEANUP

Streaming Neural Denoisers

Choose on the fly: GTCRN (ultra-fast 0.5 MB cafe cleaner), DPDFNet8 (deep music & dish clatter suppressor), VoxLock (target-speaker extraction that locks onto 1 person's voice after 3s), or OFF when nearby voices overpower your partner.

O(1) per 32 ms chunk
STAGE 04 · SPEECH GATE

Silero VAD v5

Replaces brittle volume thresholds (which trigger on fans and clattering forks) with a neural Voice Activity Detection model that opens cleanly with a 320 ms pre-roll so the first syllable is never clipped.

<0.2 ms · CPU RNN
STAGE 05 · TRANSCRIPTION

Parakeet TDT 0.6B FP16 (GPU)

Runs NVIDIA's flagship English-only FastConformer-TDT model directly on GPU FP16 Tensor Cores (plus Distil-Whisper Large-v3.5 as an optional challenger). LocalAgreement-2 locks confirmed words in bright text while keeping the last 1 to 2 trailing words amber so captions never jump.

~198 ms · 2.26 GB VRAM
STAGE 06 · GROUP TABLES

WeSpeaker CAM++ Speaker ID

When a turn finishes, a 28 MB speaker diarization network computes an L2-normalized voice embedding and clusters voices online via cosine similarity, coloring each person [SPK 1], [SPK 2], [SPK 3].

<5 ms · Online Clustering
STAGE 07 · SAFETY-GUARDED POLISH

Qwen2.5-1.5B Phonetic Fix

Runs asynchronously via llama.cpp after a sentence commits, using the last 5 turns plus your custom hotwords.txt names list to fix subtle phonetic slips. A deterministic ≤30% edit-distance guard rejects any rewrite so the AI can never invent words.

~120 ms · 1.1 GB VRAM

Proving Accuracy With Real Numbers, Not Guesswork

How do you test speech-to-text accuracy when you cannot hear the room yourself? Our trick: build a standalone Rust harness (speech-bench.exe) that takes clean speech .wav clips with exact known scripts, mathematically mixes cafe babble and restaurant music + dishes on top at calibrated Signal-to-Noise Ratios (clean, +10 dB, +5 dB, 0 dB, and -5 dB), streams them through the exact live decoder in 32 ms chunks, and scores every word's Word Error Rate (WER) and speaking-to-display latency.

Four Real Bugs Our Benchmark Caught Before We Shipped:
  1. O(N²) Re-Denoising Bottleneck: Early tests ran offline denoising on the entire growing utterance every 350 ms. Switching to O(1)-per-chunk streaming denoising with a contiguous FIFO cut overhead by ~100x.
  2. STFT Phase Comb-Filtering: Mixing 65% delayed denoised audio with 35% undelayed raw audio created a comb filter that raised WER! Pure phase-coherent modes (OFF / GTCRN / DPDF8 / VOXLOCK) fixed it immediately.
  3. Boundary Overlap Duplication: Soft-splitting long monologues at 8.5 s duplicated 3 to 4 words at the seam (13.3% fake WER). Raising the split to 14 s and adding suffix/prefix word deduplication dropped clean GTCRN WER to 1.7%.
  4. Competing Human Voices vs. Steady Noise: Single-channel denoisers excel against steady music and dish clatter, while in loud competing human babble at -5 dB, Parakeet raw (DNS: OFF) + Qwen2.5-1.5B outperformed heavy suppression (23.8% WER) because Parakeet's encoder separates overlapping speech natively.
// 05 :: ACCESSIBLE GLOSSARY

What Every Technical Term Actually Means

Speech AI documentation is full of intimidating acronyms. Here is every concept used in Speech Bridge translated into plain, everyday language, with direct links if you want to read deeper.

WER: Word Error Rate (%)
Plain English: "How many words out of 100 did the AI get wrong?"

Counts every substituted, missed, or extra word compared to the true script. 5% WER means 19 out of 20 words are spot-on (easy to follow effortlessly). 45 to 80% WER means gibberish.

SNR: Signal-to-Noise Ratio (dB)
Plain English: "How loud is the person talking compared to the room?"

+10 dB = quiet cafe. 0 dB = background music/chatter is just as loud as the person speaking to you. -5 dB = a crowded bar where background noise is noticeably louder than your conversation partner.

VAD: Voice Activity Detection
Plain English: "Is a human actually talking right now, or is that just a fork hitting a plate?"

Simple volume triggers constantly mistake HVAC hum or clattering dishes for speech. Silero VAD v5 uses a tiny neural net trained on thousands of hours of audio to recognize real human vocal chords.

LocalAgreement-2 (Anti-Flicker)
Plain English: "Do not make text jump around while my eyes are trying to read it!"

As someone speaks, the AI updates every ~350 ms. Once two back-to-back passes agree on a word, it locks in bright crisp text permanently, leaving only the last 1 to 2 unfinished words amber until complete.

FP16 Tensor Cores & VRAM
Plain English: "Using your graphics card's dedicated AI math engines."

CPUs process a few numbers at a time; modern NVIDIA GPUs have specialized Tensor Cores that multiply thousands of half-precision (FP16) neural weights at once inside ultra-fast VRAM, running large AI models ~5x faster while sipping ~9.6 Watts of GPU power.

GTCRN / DPDFNet8 vs. VoxLock
Plain English: "Cleaning room noise vs. locking onto one specific human voice."

GTCRN & DPDFNet8 strip non-speech noise (music, fans, dishes). VoxLock learns the unique vocal signature of one speaker after 3 seconds and tries to erase other humans talking at nearby tables.

Speaker Diarization (WeSpeaker)
Plain English: "Who said that around the table?"

Turns each spoken sentence into a mathematical "voice fingerprint" (embedding). Voices with similar fingerprints keep the same color badge ([SPK 1], [SPK 2], [SPK 3]) so group conversations stay readable.

WASAPI & Goertzel Spectrum
Plain English: "Low-latency Windows audio capture and a 24-bar sound visualizer."

WASAPI lets Speech Bridge read multiple microphones simultaneously with minimal delay, while the Goertzel algorithm computes 24 frequency bands (85 Hz to 7.2 kHz) in real time so you can see speech energy without hearing.

Borrow-Checker Appeasement
Plain English: "Lazily copying memory in Rust just to make compiler errors go away."

Rust enforces strict memory ownership at compile time. Rushing past it with .clone() or .to_vec() inside 32 ms audio callbacks silently wastes CPU and causes audio stutters, so we audited and eliminated every hot-path copy.

// 06 :: ENGINEERING ARCHITECTURE

Why We Chose Pure Rust Over Python (And Eliminated Borrow-Checker Debt)

Most AI speech demos are built in Python with PyTorch, Conda environments, and Electron frontends. For a daily accessibility tool that has to open instantly in a noisy coffee shop and run for hours on laptop battery without glitching, native Rust wins decisively.

✓ Pure Rust + Native Win32 + Dynamic CUDA FFI

  • 630 KB Executable: Compiles to a single tiny native Win32 binary with zero browser/Electron overhead and instant cold launch.
  • Zero Python Version Hell: Dynamically loads prebuilt C-API shared libraries (sherpa-onnx-c-api.dll) via Win32 LoadLibraryW, working cleanly even on machines with Python 3.14 where PyTorch wheels often break.
  • Deterministic Real-Time Audio: No Garbage Collector pauses mid-sentence. Fixed [i16; 512] stack arrays cross the WASAPI audio thread with zero heap allocations.
  • 100% Shared Live & Benchmark Code: Both the GUI (speech-bridge.exe) and WAV+noise CLI tester (speech-bench.exe) link the exact same Rust StreamDecoder library.

✕ Typical Python / PyTorch / Electron Stack

  • 3 to 5 GB Environment Sprawl: Requires pinned Python 3.11/3.12 virtualenvs, pip/Conda dependency trees, and fragile wheel compilation on Windows.
  • Slow Startup & Heavy Idle RAM: Importing PyTorch + Electron routinely eats 800 MB to 1.5 GB RAM and 5 to 12 seconds before the window even appears.
  • GC & GIL Audio Jitter: Python's Global Interpreter Lock and garbage collector can stall real-time 32 ms microphone callbacks unless pushed into C extensions anyway.
  • Drift Between Prototype & App: Often leads to separate Python test scripts that behave slightly differently from the production desktop UI.

Zero-Allocation Ownership Audit (Eliminating Borrow-Checker Appeasement)

AI-assisted Rust code often slips in subtle .clone() and .to_vec() calls to appease the borrow checker. We audited every file in src/ (verified 0 uses of Rc<RefCell>, Box::leak, or static mut) and removed all 4 real hot-path allocations:

01 · src/meter.rs (Real-Time Audio Thread)
Replaced SyncSender<Vec<i16>> + chunk.to_vec() every 32 ms inside the WASAPI callback with stack-allocated [i16; 512] Copy frames (0 heap allocations in OS audio callbacks).
02 · src/stream_decoder.rs (ASR Buffer)
Replaced per-decode Vec<f32> clones (~640 KB x 3/sec) with a single reusable asr_scratch: Vec<f32> filled in place + copy_within overlap trims.
03 · src/stream_decoder.rs (Idle Pre-Roll)
Replaced VecDeque<Vec<f32>> + idle hp_f32.clone() with fixed [f32; 512] stack rings and in-place enhance_stream_chunk_into().
04 · src/ui.rs (30 Hz Meter Repaints)
Eliminated active_mic_name.clone() on every 30 Hz meter paint tick by formatting directly into a reusable health_buf: String under the short lock.
