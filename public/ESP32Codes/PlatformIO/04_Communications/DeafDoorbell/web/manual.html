Quick Start: Ready in 3 Minutes

Plug & Play

Your system comes with two types of devices: the Doorbell Sensor (Leader) that listens near your existing chime, and one or more Light Displays (Followers) that light up around your house.

  • 1

    Plug in the Devices

    Plug the Doorbell Sensor into USB power near your doorbell chime box. Plug your Light Displays into standard USB power in the rooms where you want alerts (such as the living room, kitchen, or office).

  • 2

    Connect with Your Smartphone or Computer

    Open Wi-Fi settings on your smartphone or computer and connect to the Doorbell Sensor's network:

    NETWORK NAME (SSID):
    DoorbellDetectorSystem
    PASSWORD:
    shrek1234!
  • 3

    Open the Control Page

    Open any web browser and go to http://192.168.4.1 (or http://deafdoorbell.local). The control page will open automatically without needing home internet access.

  • 4

    Try a Test Flash

    Click the big blue button labeled "Test Flash". Your Light Displays will immediately light up across the room with zero noticeable delay.

ⓘ
Have Two Doorbell Sensors in Your Kit? (e.g., Front & Back Door):
Both sensors initially share the default Wi-Fi name DoorbellDetectorSystem. To easily configure each door without Wi-Fi confusion, set them up one at a time:
  1. Plug in Sensor 1 (Front Door) first. Connect to DoorbellDetectorSystem on your smartphone or computer.
  2. Open http://192.168.4.1, scroll to Wi-Fi Hotspot, rename it to Doorbell_FrontDoor, and save.
  3. Now plug in Sensor 2 (Back Door). Connect to DoorbellDetectorSystem, rename it to Doorbell_BackDoor, and set its zone to Area 2 (Back Door).
Now both door sensors have distinct, permanent Wi-Fi networks!

Easy Pairing Guide

Quick Setup

Pairing locks your Light Displays to your own Doorbell Sensor using a private house key. This ensures that even if your next-door neighbor also uses DeafDoorbell, their doorbell will never trigger your lights.

Physical Button Guide (Bottom of Unit)
ⓘ Accidental press? If you accidentally press the left Reset button, don't worry! It simply restarts the unit safely in 1 second without erasing any pairing or settings.
  • 1

    Put the Light Display in Pairing Mode

    Look at the bottom of the Light Display unit where the USB cable enters. Locate the small black button on the RIGHT side of the USB port (the Pairing button shown in the diagram above). Press and hold it down for 3 seconds until the lights start gently pulsing in orange concentric rings.

  • 2

    Click Open Pairing on Your Phone or Computer

    Open the control page (http://192.168.4.1), scroll down to the Follower Pairing card, and click the green button: "Open Pairing Mode (60s)".

  • 3

    Done! Watch for the Green Flash

    The units will automatically find each other within two seconds. The Light Display will flash solid green for 1 second to let you know it is securely saved.

✔
Permanent Memory: Once paired, the devices remember each other forever, even if unplugged or during power outages.

Using Multiple Doorbells (Multi-Door Setup)

Multi-Zone
Can a light display connect to more than one doorbell sensor?
YES! A Light Display can easily respond to multiple Doorbell Sensors.
If your home has a front door and a back door (or side gate), you can place a sensor at each chime.

Back Door Sensor

House Code: Shared
Zone: Area 2 (Back Door)

How to Set Up Multiple Doors:

  1. Set Distinct Wi-Fi Hotspots: Power up your front door sensor first and rename its hotspot to Doorbell_FrontDoor under the Wi-Fi card. Then power up your back door sensor and rename its hotspot to Doorbell_BackDoor. Now you can easily connect to either door's settings page anytime.
  2. Share the Same House Code: In your Front Door Sensor's control page, copy its 4-character House Key (for example, A48F). Open your Back Door Sensor's control page and type in the exact same House Key so all sensors speak the same language.
  3. Assign Each Door a Zone: Set your front door sensor to Area 1 (Front Door) and your back door sensor to Area 2 (Back Door).
  4. Pick Which Lights React: In your control page follower table, you can choose:
    • All Trigger Areas (Default): The light flashes whenever either door rings.
    • Front Door Only: The light will only flash when visitors arrive at the front door.
    • Back Door Only: Perfect for a light display in the kitchen, back patio, or garage.

Resetting Wi-Fi on the Doorbell Sensor

Find the small black button on the RIGHT side of the USB port (the BOOT button) on the Doorbell Sensor and hold it down for 3 seconds:

  • The small blue light flashes rapidly 10 times.
  • Wi-Fi name resets to: DoorbellDetectorSystem
  • Wi-Fi password resets to: shrek1234!
  • Safe Reset: Your sound sensitivity calibrations and paired lights are kept safe and are not erased.

Unpairing a Light Display

Find the small black button on the RIGHT side of the USB port (the Pairing button) on the Light Display and hold it down for 3 seconds:

  • The light unlinks from your previous sensor.
  • The lights begin gently pulsing in orange concentric rings.
  • Ready to be paired to a new doorbell sensor.

Common Questions & Troubleshooting

Help Center
My lights do not flash when someone rings the doorbell.
Check the Doorbell Sensor placement. Make sure it is close to your chime box. If your chime is quiet, open the control page (http://192.168.4.1) and adjust the Sound Sensitivity slider slightly so it picks up softer sounds.
The lights flash on loud talking, dogs barking, or TV noise.
Open the control page and slightly raise the Sound Sensitivity slider. Doorbell chimes have a sharp, resonant strike, whereas voices and television audio are lower in volume. Moving the slider just a little higher easily filters out everyday room chatter.
I forgot my custom Wi-Fi password.
Hold the small black button nearest the USB power cable on the Doorbell Sensor for 3 seconds. The blue light will blink 10 times, restoring the Wi-Fi name to DoorbellDetectorSystem and password to shrek1234!.
A Light Display is glowing with orange rings and won't flash.
The orange rings mean the light is in Pairing Mode waiting for a sensor. Connect to your Doorbell Sensor's control page (http://192.168.4.1), scroll to the Follower Pairing section, and click "Open Pairing Mode (60s)". The light will flash green and be ready.
Does this need home internet or a cloud subscription?
No! DeafDoorbell is 100% private and subscription-free. It uses direct, instantaneous local wireless signals between units that work seamlessly without an internet connection.

Technical Appendix

For Makers & Engineers
⚠ Developer Note: This section is just for curious tech enthusiasts, hobbyists, and developers. You do not need any of this technical information for everyday setup or operation!

Hardware Architecture & GPIO Pinouts

Both nodes are built on the ESP32-C3 SuperMini (RISC-V single-core 160MHz) running Arduino ESP-IDF with zero modem sleep (WIFI_PS_NONE) on Wi-Fi Channel 1:

Device Pin / Interface Connected Component Technical Description
Doorbell Sensor GPIO 3 (ADC1_CH3) MAX4466 Analog Mic Output Continuous 64-sample chunking at ~20kHz sample rate.
Doorbell Sensor GPIO 4 Soft Ground for Mic Driven solid LOW to provide ultra-clean ground reference.
Doorbell Sensor GPIO 8 Onboard Status Blue LED Inverted PWM channel tracking real-time sound levels.
Doorbell Sensor GPIO 9 Onboard BOOT Button Active LOW with internal pullup; 3s hold restores Wi-Fi defaults.
Light Display GPIO 5 WS2812B 8x8 LED Matrix High-speed non-blocking FastLED bitbang data stream.
Light Display GPIO 9 Onboard BOOT Button Active LOW; 3s hold unbinds NVS network key to re-enter pairing.

Acoustic Detection Algorithm (MAD & Debounce)

Rather than basic peak-to-peak amplitude which easily triggers on ambient noise, the firmware calculates the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) over single-pass 64-sample chunks (~3.2ms window) combined with a software IIR DC-blocking filter:

outSample = inSample - lastInput + (0.95f * lastOutput);
madLevel = (sumOfAbsoluteDifferences / 64) * ANALOG_MAD_SCALE;

A multi-chunk leaky integrator debounces the audio across consecutive windows (default 2 chunks = ~6.4ms) to distinguish instant chime resonance from brief contact clicks.

Task Watchdog Timer (WDT) & Self-Healing

To guarantee 24/7 uptime without freezing, the Doorbell Sensor initializes the ESP32 hardware Task Watchdog Timer with a 5-second panic timeout (esp_task_wdt_init(5, true)). The timer is fed each cycle. If an unexpected radio driver lockup ever stalls execution, the chip automatically resets within 5 seconds and records the reset reason.

Flight Recorder & Diagnostics CSV

Every acoustic event is logged in a rolling in-memory flight recorder. Developers can inspect live telemetry at /log.html or export detailed millisecond oscilloscope traces via deafdoorbell_diagnostics.csv.
