### II. THE IMPEDANCE TRIALS

The first three attempts nearly rendered his motor cortex permanently non-functional.

Tidus had dragged himself onto the black volcanic shingle of Besaid’s southern cove. The induction tap behind his ear was no longer enough. The Al Bhed salvage ship had sailed into the southern fog, taking the thirty-two-register mechanical processor with it. 

The moment the wireless gap exceeded four meters, the arithmetic load had slammed back into his biological brain like a collapsing retaining wall. The migraine was back, worse than before: twenty thousand lines of Spiran cosmological calculus grinding against his frontal lobe, generating enough internal heat that his teeth felt loose in his gums.

He needed mobile registers. He needed wetware with lower electrical impedance than the copper coils he had scavenged from the beach debris.

He caught his first test subject in the tide pools: an adolescent Sahagin, pinned beneath a fallen volcanic slab. He used a sharpened iron salvage pry-bar to excise the creature’s auxiliary cephalic cluster—the secondary ganglion located beneath the creature’s dorsal crest that processed deep-water echolocation and pressure gradients.

**Trial 01:**
He spliced three inches of the Sahagin’s main optic nerve directly into his left cervical plexus using bare copper wire.
*Result:* Catastrophic failure. The Sahagin’s nervous system ran on an electrochemical acetylcholine gradient, while his own dream-stuff matrix operated on high-frequency pyrefly resonance. The mismatch resulted in an immediate electrical back-emf. A high-voltage shock surged directly into his spinal cord. His left arm went rigid, seizing into claw-like flexion; his vision flashed violently into blinding emerald static, and he collapsed onto the shingle, his heels drumming involuntarily against the wet black pebbles for twenty minutes while his brain rebooted.

**Trial 02:**
He attempted to introduce an intermediate step: a brass step-down transformer salvaged from a broken navigational buoy, wired between the ganglion and his occipital nerve.
*Result:* Thermal runaway. The transformer overheated within four minutes. The organic ganglion literally boiled, filling the sea cove with the stench of charred fish and scorched enamel. The copper wires melted into his neck, fusing the burnt biological matter into his dream-flesh.

He did not scream. He simply sat on the sand, pulled the smoking wires from his neck with pliers—watching the pyreflies knit the torn skin closed without bleeding—and picked up his notebook.

`IMPEDANCE MISMATCH: 440 OHMS // DELTA-V: FATAL TO CARBON BIOLOGY`  
`NOTE: DREAM-SUBSTRATE DOES NOT EXTINGUISH UPON CURRENT SURGE`  
`HYPOTHESIS: ORGANIC GANGLION REQUIRES ACTIVE PYREFLY CARRIER MODULATION`

**Trial 04:**
On the third day, among the ruins of a drowned machina pump station at the base of the Besaid waterfall, he discovered the key.

He didn't just wire the nerve. He used a harvested core crystal from a water fiend as a capacitive bridge, bathing the severed Sahagin brain-tissue in a constant, low-amperage current derived from his own dream-flesh. 

The pyreflies in his body recognized the frequency. They reached out from his severed dermal layer like tiny glowing nematodes, threading themselves through the Sahagin’s biological nerve cells, stabilizing the rotting proteins, preventing decay, and synchronizing the creature’s firing potential with his own thoughts.

A cold, glassy click sounded inside his head.

His field of vision doubled. Suddenly, without turning his head, he could perceive electromagnetic currents in the water and the pressure fluctuations of the wind against the cliffs.

More importantly: sixteen percent of the screaming equations in his skull shifted into the harvested ganglion. The pressure in his temples dropped. The agony smoothed out into a dull, manageable vibration.

Tidus stood up from the wet rocks.

Beneath the collar of his tattered yellow shirt, three thick, insulated copper conduits now ran from the base of his skull down into a brass junction box bolted directly into the connective tissue of his upper spine. He had scavenged a heavy iron cooling fin from a marine engine and secured it along his cervical vertebrae to radiate away the thermal waste.

He was not a human boy anymore. He was an under-cooled prototype.

He turned toward the interior of the island.

### IV. THE CAUSEWAY

The fog off the Moonflow was thick, smelling of rotting reeds and marsh gas.

The wooden causeway creaked beneath the heavy boots of the guardians. Kimahri walked at the vanguard, his massive ronso frame tense, his blue ears twitching toward the mist. His hand gripped his halberd so tightly that the sinews of his forearms stood out like ropes.

Behind him, Wakka wiped sweat from his eyes, his fingers rolling his blitzball with nervous, erratic twitches.

*"Quiet today,"* Wakka muttered, trying for bravado and failing completely. *"Usually the shoopuf trail is crawling with bite bugs and ochus. Not even a chirp out here."*

*"Quiet is not peace,"* Lulu said coldly. She cradled a small, stitched moogle doll in her left arm, her right hand resting near the vials of distilled elemental essence in her skirt. Her eyes never left the treeline. *"Look at the water, Wakka."*

Wakka glanced over the wooden railing.

Floating in the stagnant shallows were three large armored fiends—heavy swamp basilisks. Their bellies were slit open with long, ruler-straight incisions. Their skulls had been cored like apples. The brains and main spinal cords were cleanly excised.

*"By Yevon..."* Wakka whispered, stepping back. *"Who does that? Even the Al Bhed don't eat fiend heads!"*

At the center of the procession, Yuna stopped. Her summoner’s staff clinked faintly against the wooden boards. Beside her, Auron walked with his left arm tucked into his scarlet haori. He hadn't spoken since they crossed the river. His face was shadowed beneath his collar, his hand resting on the hilt of his heavy cleaver.

Then the sound came.

*Krrrrk... ch-ch-ch-ch... hummmmmm.*

It was low, rhythmic, vibrating through the wet planks of the causeway before it was heard in the air. A mechanical grinding sound, like an iron millstone turning on ungreased bearings.

*"Shields!"* Lulu snapped, her voice cutting through the fog.

From the murky reed beds to the left of the bridge, the reeds parted.

It was roughly humanoid, but its proportions were monstrously wrong.

The figure stood nearly seven feet tall, hunched forward beneath a heavy dorsal carapace made of scavenged brass engine casings, curved copper piping, and bolted iron plates. Protruding from the left side of its skull was an auxiliary sensory mast—a cluster of three Sahagin optic lobes mounted in a brass housing, twitching independently toward every member of the party. Along its exposed neck and shoulders, the skin was pale, waxy, and stitched with thick insulated copper wire; underneath the waxy translucence, dense clusters of glowing cyan pyreflies pulsed in rapid, erratic rhythms.

In its right hand, it dragged a heavy pneumatic cutting torch connected by a braided steel hose to a pressurized canister on its hip.

*"Fiend!"* Wakka roared, panic overriding his training. He wound back his arm and hurled the spiked blitzball with all his strength.

The heavy leather sphere whistled through the air, striking the creature squarely in the chest.

There was a dull, metallic clang.

The creature did not flinch. The ball did not knock it back. Instead, a pair of articulated copper baffles on the creature's chest snapped shut around the sphere, absorbing the kinetic energy through a miniature hydraulic damper. The ball was dropped onto the mud with a wet slap.

*"Kimahri strike!"*

The ronso launched himself across the causeway, his halberd descending in an overhead cleave capable of splitting an armored scale-beast in two.

The White-Iron Fiend moved.

Its movement was horrifying because it possessed zero biological inertia. It did not shift its weight; its dream-stuff limbs simply snapped into a new geometric orientation. It brought up its left forearm—an assembly of forged iron struts wrapped around its pale wrist—and caught the descending steel halberd with a bone-jarring shriek of metal on metal.

Kimahri grunted, putting all eight hundred pounds of his ronso muscle into the thrust.

The creature’s auxiliary eyes twitched toward Kimahri’s face. From a small speaker grille welded beneath its chin, a burst of acoustic static buzzed:

`PHYSICAL FORCE: 3.8 kN // RONSO MUSCULAR EFFICIENCY: ESTIMATED 42% // SUB-OPTIMAL`

*"Firaga!"* Lulu screamed, stepping forward, her hands tracing glowing crimson runes in the damp air.

A sphere of roaring, superheated plasma detonated across the creature's back. The blast tore through the reeds, setting the marsh water boiling in a geyser of white steam.

When the smoke cleared, the creature was still standing.

A series of copper heat-pipes along its spine were glowing cherry-red, venting superheated steam from miniature relief valves along its shoulder blades. The fire magic had not incinerated its meat; it had simply pegged the thermal gauges on its dorsal chassis.

Kimahri was thrown backward by a sudden, brutal burst of pneumatic force from the creature's elbow actuator, crashing into the wooden railing.

Yuna stepped past her fallen guardian. Her face was pale, her summoner's robes spattered with marsh water. She lifted her staff, her voice ringing out in clear, desperate prayer:

*"Hear me, great beast of Besaid! Protector of the innocent! Valefor!"*

### V. THE HARVEST OF THE AEON

The sky above the causeway tore open with a flash of holy white light.

With a majestic, piercing cry, the great avian aeon descended from the clouds, its massive wings of white and crimson feathers displacing the marsh fog in a single, thunderous gale. Its talons were extended, glowing with divine energy.

The White-Iron Fiend stopped.

All four of its optical assemblies—the two biological eyes beneath its forehead, and the two mounted in brass housings on its temple—locked onto the descending aeon.

The creature’s posture completely changed. The defensive, mechanical posture vanished. It lowered its cutting torch. Its copper cooling fins flared open.

*"...What is that?"*

The voice from the speaker was thin, cracked, carrying the ghostly cadence of a boy who had spent his life near salt water, yet distorted by mechanical oscillation.

*"It’s not flesh. It’s not... a fiend."*

Valefor struck.

The aeon’s talons plunged toward the creature’s chest. But the Fiend did not dodge. It lunged forward, throwing both of its iron-clad arms upward, willingly impaling its own forearms onto the aeon's talons.

The holy light tore through the creature's dream-flesh. Clear pyrefly fluid sprayed across the wooden bridge.

The Fiend did not scream. It let out a sharp, breathless gasp of pure, ecstatic comprehension.

`INPUT VECTOR: REFINED PYREFLY COHERENCE // FREQUENCY: 144.2 MHz`  
`ORDER DEGREE: 99.8% // HARMONIC STABILITY: ABSOLUTE`  
`IT IS NOT DISSIPATING // IT HAS AN EXTERNAL ANCHOR`

*"It's an external projection,"* the Fiend whispered, its tone manic, frantic, shivering with delight. *"A standing wave driven by an active transmitter!"*

From its hip harness, the Fiend drew two heavy, three-pronged iron spikes wrapped in thick coils of silver wire—harmonic dampening clamps salvaged from a sunken power station.

With blinding, mechanical savagery, the Fiend drove the first iron clamp directly through Valefor’s left wing joint, pinning the joint to the wooden floor of the causeway.

Valefor shrieked—a high, agonized sound that shook the leaves of the marsh trees.

*"No!"* Yuna cried out, tears spilling down her cheeks. She raised her staff, her hands shaking violently as she initiated the dismissal rite. *"Valefor! Return! Return to the temple!"*

The aeon's body began to shimmer. Its feathers turned translucent, beginning the process of dissolving into holy pyreflies to return to the Fayth.

*"No, you don't,"* Tidus rasped.

He lunged onto the pinned aeon's back. His hands moved in a blur of terrifying competence. He drove the second silver-wrapped stake into the base of the aeon’s skull, where the divine crest met the spinal column.

He tapped a dial on his wrist.

`CARRIER LOCK: ESTABLISHED`  
`PHASE INTERFERENCE: ACTIVE // BANDWIDTH CLAMP: 100%`  
`DISMISSAL HANDSHAKE: BLOCKED`

The dissolving process froze.

Valefor remained trapped on the bridge—half-solid, half-transparent, frozen in a state of suspended, screaming existence. The holy pyreflies could not dissipate; the harmonic clamp was bouncing the dismissal signal back on itself, creating an artificial standing-wave trap.

*"It worked,"* Tidus breathed, his waxy face inches from the aeon's golden eye. He was trembling with scientific fervor. *"The carrier wave is trapped in the local circuit. It can't return to the server."*

He turned.

For the first time, he looked directly at the party.

His gaze swept over Wakka, who was trembling on his knees; over Lulu, whose hands were frozen in mid-spell; over Kimahri, struggling to rise through broken ribs.

Then his eyes met Yuna’s.

Yuna was staring at her staff. She was chanting the sacred dismissal prayers over and over, her knuckles white, her voice breaking into sobs:

*"Return... please, Valefor, return... why won't you return...?"*

The Fiend tilted its head. One of its auxiliary optic lenses clicked forward, focusing on the summoner's tears.

*"You are the transmitter,"* Tidus said. The speaker grille hissed with static. *"Your nervous system is broadcasting the clock signal. How? What is the antenna?"*

He reached toward her with a gore-streaked, brass-jointed hand.

*"RUN!"*

Lulu threw herself in front of Yuna, dragging the weeping girl backward along the causeway toward the ferry landing. Wakka grabbed Kimahri’s shoulder, hauling the massive ronso up, and the four of them sprinted into the thick fog, their boots pounding against the wooden planks in blind, desperate terror.

Tidus did not follow.

He did not care about the summoner’s flesh. He looked down at the trapped aeon pinned beneath his knees. The divine bird was shuddering, its eyes wide with unnatural terror, trapped between life and memory.

Tidus picked up his cables.

*"Let's see,"* he murmured, his fingers gently tracing the silver stakes driven into the aeon's skull. *"Let's see what you're made of."*

### VI. THE ROAD TO DJOSE (THREE DAYS LATER)

The rain was cold.

The surviving party had reached the high stone steps of Djose Temple. They were shivering, soaked, hollow-eyed.

Yuna stood at the edge of the terrace, looking back down into the misty valley they had fled. Her hands were clasped in front of her chest, her fingers raw from praying.

*"Try again, Lady Yuna,"* Wakka said quietly, his voice broken. *"Maybe... maybe it just needs time. It was just a strong fiend, right? Aeons don't die. They're sacred."*

Yuna closed her eyes. She lifted her arms, drawing breath into her chest, and whistled the clear, high note that had called Valefor since she was a little girl in Besaid.

She held the note.

The air remained dead.

There was no holy wind. There was no white light. The pyreflies in the courtyard did not gather; they simply drifted across the flagstones, cold and inert.

Deep inside her chest, where the warm, comforting resonance of the Besaid Fayth had lived since her pilgrimage began, there was only a horrific, flat void.

And then, as she lowered her trembling hands, the wind shifted from the valley below.

Faint, distant, carried across three leagues of marsh and mud, came a sound:

*Krrrrk... ch-ch-ch-ch... hummmmmm.*

The grinding hum had resumed. And it was moving north.
