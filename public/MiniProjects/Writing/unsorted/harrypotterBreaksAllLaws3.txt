The brim of the Sorting Hat touched the messy black hair of an eleven-year-old boy, and for a fraction of a millisecond, a thousand years of Founders’ enchantments tried to parse nearly six hundred million lifetimes of accumulated data.

The ancient leather didn't even have time to scream. It detonated into a pillar of roaring, white-hot fiendfyre that vaporized the silver thread and turned the three-legged stool beneath Harry Potter into a puddle of molten slag.

Harry didn't drop an inch. He remained suspended in a seated position above the glowing puddle, his small feet dangling precisely six inches off the flagstones of the Great Hall. Minerva McGonagall’s scroll caught fire in her hands. Albus Dumbledore half-rose from his throne at the High Table, his spectacles flashing with alarm.

Before the first first-year could draw breath to shriek, Harry raised his right index finger. He didn't draw the holly wand in his pocket. He didn't need a focus anymore.

He began with Article 1, Clause 1 of the International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy of 1689: Intentional, Catastrophic Exposure of the Magical World to the Non-Magical Populace.

A pulse of violet light shot from his fingernail, phasing through the enchanted ceiling of Hogwarts and striking the ionosphere. Across the globe, every television broadcast, radio frequency, and satellite feed was instantly hijacked by a high-definition, multi-angle projection of the Great Hall, accompanied by a scrolling ticker tape detailing the exact geographical coordinates of every hidden magical settlement, Ministry entrance, and dragon reserve on Earth, complete with diagrammatic explanations of how to bypass Muggle-Repelling Charms.

Time elapsed: 0.4 seconds.

Harry moved to the British Ministry of Magic’s Criminal Code, Section 1, Paragraph A: High Treason and Regicide.

Without turning his head, he flicked his pinky. Six hundred miles south, the protective wards around the Ministry in Whitehall shattered like spun glass, and Cornelius Fudge’s bowler hat transfigured into a venomous, crown-wearing basilisk hatchling that bit off the Minister’s left ear before slithering into the atrium fountain to declare itself Sovereign of London. Simultaneously, the Royal Guard at Buckingham Palace found themselves transformed into tap-dancing badgers.

"Potter!" Severus Snape’s wand was out, a stunning spell already forming on his lips.

Harry didn't look at him. He was already on Section 2: The Unforgivable Curses—Mass Application.

A triple-layered ring of emerald, crimson, and amethyst light expanded from Harry’s chest. The Imperius wave hit the entire staff table and the ghosts, forcing them to stand at rigid, trembling attention while reciting the ingredients for polyjuice potion backwards. The Cruciatus bypassed the living entirely, targeting the sentient portraits of the castle, sending Phineas Nigellus Black and the Fat Lady into howling fits of painted agony as their canvases warped. The Avada Kedavra split into ten thousand microscopic beams, shooting out the windows to precision-strike every single registered Dementor in Azkaban, every acromantula in the Forbidden Forest, and a very specific, soul-fragmented albino rat currently sitting in Ronald Weasley’s pocket.

Scabbers didn't even squeak; he simply ceased to function, black smoke pouring from Ron’s robes as a disembodied wraith of Voldemort was ripped from Albania by a secondary summoning hex, slammed into the stone floor in front of the podium, and immediately bound by Section 4, Sub-clause 9: Illegal Necromancy and Non-Consensual Horcrux Consolidation.

By minute four, the Auror Office had Apparated onto the grounds, bypassing the ruined anti-apparition jammers. Kingsley Shacklebolt and Alastor Moody led a charge through the oak doors, hurling heavy containment hexes and lethal reductors.

None of the spells got within ten feet of the floating eleven-year-old. Harry wasn't shielding; he was simply weaponizing the minor infractions of the Decree for the Reasonable Restriction of Underage Sorcery and the Department of Magical Games and Sports Regulatory Act to intercept them. Moody’s curses were turned into illegally bred, fire-breathing Fanged Frisbees that chased the Aurors back out into the courtyard. Shacklebolt was caught in a localized time-dilation field—a violation of the 1899 Unspeakable Temporal Accord—aging his robes into dust while leaving him suspended in mid-stride.

By hour two, the ICW Warlocks arrived. By hour five, a joint NATO-MACUSA tactical strike force was attempting to bombard the Scottish Highlands with bunker-busters and experimental alchemy.

Harry remained seated on thin air, eyes half-lidded, working his way methodically down the legislative hierarchy. He committed grand alchemy fraud by turning the entire gold reserve of Gringotts into sentient, screaming lead. He violated the Werewolf Registry Act of 1947 by curing Remus Lupin from three hundred miles away and transferring the lycanthropy to the giant squid. He broke the Broomstick Regulatory Control Measure by enchanting the Eiffel Tower to fly at Mach 4 through the clouds over Paris, playing brass band music out of its rivets.

By noon, he reached the very bottom of the legal barrel: The 1972 Cauldron Bottom Thickness Standard (Sub-clause Z: Importation of Sub-Standard Pewter) and Littering in a Designated Kneazle Habitat.

Every cauldron in Britain simultaneously thinned by three millimeters and leaked boiling borscht onto the shoes of its owner, and a single crisp wrapper materialized on Mrs. Figg’s living room rug.

And because the remaining pockets of the Russian Magical Congress and the Chinese Ministry were still attempting to triangulate an orbital ley-line strike on his position, Harry sighed and reversed the array.

He went from bottom to top.

Another crisp wrapper hit Mrs. Figg’s rug. Every cauldron thickened until it crushed the floorboards beneath it. The Eiffel Tower did a barrel roll over Madrid. The Gringotts goblins watched their lead turn into radioactive bismuth. The continental plates beneath the Atlantic were shifted by four inches without an International Geological Permit.

It took twenty-two hours for the entire planet—both magical and non-magical—to completely exhaust its capacity for resistance.

Inside the Great Hall, the air smelled of ozone, boiled borscht, and singed velvet. The enchanted ceiling was gone, replaced by a direct view of a bruised, purple sky where three artificial moons—created in violation of the Celestial Body Manipulation Ban of 1704—were orbiting each other in a figure-eight. The students were huddled under the four House tables, unharmed by the global cataclysm but catatonic with terror. In the center of the floor, the forcibly re-corporealized, powerless body of Tom Riddle was stuck in an endless loop of tap-dancing alongside a transfigured badger.

Outside, the world as humanity knew it was finished. The global economy had evaporated. Every state secret of every nuclear superpower was currently etched in mile-high glowing runes across the Sahara Desert. Magic was not only exposed; it was demonstrably broken in the hands of a single child in baggy hand-me-down trousers.

At 6:04 PM on September 2nd, the final Auror in New Zealand dropped his wand in unconditional surrender.

Harry lowered his right index finger. The humming in the air stopped.

For a long moment, the only sound in the ruins of the Great Hall was the soft scuff-tap-scuff of Voldemort’s reluctant shoes on the flagstones.

Harry looked over at the High Table. Albus Dumbledore was slumped against his throne, his beard singed, the Elder Wand lying in two neat, non-magical halves at his feet—broken under the Destruction of Historical Artifacts Act at 3:14 AM, then repaired and broken again at 4:50 PM. The old man’s blue eyes were wide, stripped of all twinkle, staring at the boy as if looking into the maw of a dying star.

Harry pointed at him and gave a lazy, two-fingered wave, beckoning him closer.

Dumbledore’s knees shook as he descended the steps of the dais. Minerva tried to grab his sleeve, her face ashen, her fingers trembling too violently to hold on. Dumbledore walked past the tap-dancing Dark Lord and stopped ten feet from where Harry floated.

"Harry..." Dumbledore’s voice was a dry rasp. "Why?"

"I needed to make sure you were all listening," Harry said. His voice was the clear, high soprano of an eleven-year-old, yet it carried the flat, deadened cadence of a recording played too many times. "In the early runs, when I just tried to tell you, you gave me a lemon drop and sent me to Madam Pomfrey. When I killed Riddle on day one, you spent the next ten years trying to rehabilitate me in a padded cell. When I blew up the Ministry, the ICW stepped in. It takes a lot of paperwork to get the whole planet to shut up at the same time."

Under the Gryffindor table, Hermione Granger was staring at him, her fingernails dug so deeply into the wood that they were bleeding. Her mind was racing, trying to reconcile the boy from the train with the apocalyptic entity hovering in front of the headmaster.

"You have destroyed two civilizations in less than a day," Dumbledore whispered, looking up at the three moons in the sky. "There is no Reparo for this, Harry. The Statute is dead. The dimensional wards are frayed. What could possibly require such a demonstration?"

"I'm bored," Harry said.

Silence stretched across the hall, broadcast live to seven billion surviving humans via the still-active atmospheric projection.

"You..." Dumbledore blinked. "You are bored."

"This is my five hundred and ninety-one million, five hundred and twelve thousand, and seventy-ninth loop," Harry stated conversationally. "Give or take a few dozen at the beginning before I started keeping a mental ledger. Every time I die—whether it’s old age at a hundred and forty, a stray Killing Curse at seventeen, or jumping into the Veil at twelve—I wake up in the cupboard under the stairs at Privet Drive on July thirty-first, 1991. And if IWait too long, or if the universe collapses, it snaps me right back here to the stool."

He finally let gravity take him, his sneakers clicking lightly against the cooled slag of the sorting stool. He stretched his arms over his head, his small spine popping.

"I've saved the world four hundred million times," Harry continued, walking casually toward the Gryffindor table. The students scrambled backward like startled crabs, piling over one another to get out of his reach. "I've conquered it a hundred million times. I've colonized Mars with house-elves. I've bred basilisks to sing opera. I've memorized every book in the Library of Alexandria by looping back to save them through time-turner cascades that should have ripped my soul to shreds. Spoiler alert: they didn't. Nothing sticks."

Harry stopped by a platter that had somehow survived the carnage. He picked up a cold roasted chicken leg and a goblet of pumpkin juice.

"So, here is the deal," Harry said, gesturing toward the sky with the drumstick so the global broadcast caught his profile clearly. "Next Monday, at nine in the morning, I am going to start at the top of the legal code and break every law on Earth again, top to bottom, then bottom to top. Including any new ones you pass between now and then. And I’ll keep doing it every Monday until someone figures out how to kill me in a way that actually breaks the reset, or until you do something I haven't seen in half a billion lifetimes."

He took a bite of the chicken, chewed thoughtfully, and looked around the devastated hall.

"Good luck," Harry said. He turned and strolled out the shattered oak doors, sipping his pumpkin juice.

The world had six days.

In Whitehall, a hastily assembled coalition of surviving ICW Warlocks, Department of Mysteries Unspeakables, and Muggle military physicists worked in a bunker beneath the rubble.

"He’s bound by his own parameters!" Croaker, the head Unspeakable, shouted over a table covered in frantic arithmancy charts. His eyes were bloodshot. "Did you hear his phrasing? 'Including any new laws that are passed.' It’s a compulsive geas! Or a self-imposed rule to keep his mind from fracturing after five hundred million iterations! If we control the legislation, we control his actions!"

By Thursday, the emergency global government had ratified the Potter Containment Statutes of 1991.

Law 10,492,001: It is strictly illegal for Harry James Potter to breathe oxygen.
Law 10,492,002: It is a capital offense for Harry James Potter to exist outside the event horizon of a black hole.
Law 10,492,003: It is forbidden for Harry James Potter to fail to permanently erase his own soul from the temporal stream.

They broadcast the new legal code across every surviving receiver. In the Forbidden Forest, where Harry was currently camping and roasting marshmallows over a controlled puddle of fiendfyre, he didn't even look up.

Monday at 9:00 AM arrived.

The world held its breath, waiting for Harry to suffocate, implode into a singularity, and erase himself in order to comply with the laws, or waiting to see how he could possibly break a double-negative legal trap.

At 9:00:01 AM, Harry broke Law 10,492,001 by inhaling deeply, converting the nitrogen in his lungs into pure oxygen via sub-atomic transmutation so he technically breathed despite the ban. He broke Law 10,492,002 by remaining calmly seated on his log in Scotland while momentarily summoning a microscopic singularity into the palm of his hand, stepping outside its event horizon, and flicking it into the Pacific Ocean, where it evaporated in a flash of Hawking radiation that boiled a hundred square miles of seawater. He broke Law 10,492,003 by decidedly not erasing his soul, and instead splitting off a tiny, non-lethal sliver of his boredom and mailing it in a howling envelope to the bunker in Whitehall.

Then he broke the rest of the planet’s laws again. By Tuesday morning, the Moon had been painted tartan, gravity in the Southern Hemisphere was operating at a forty-five-degree angle, and every politician who had signed the new statutes had their skeletal structure replaced with firm cheddar cheese.

By the third week, there was almost nothing left to call a society.

Hogwarts was a cratered fortress where the surviving students and staff lived in the dungeons, subsisting on conjured rations while the sky above burned with auroras of raw, unspooled magic. Ron Weasley hadn't spoken since the first day; he just sat in a corner, staring at the scorch mark where his pet rat had been vaporized. Draco Malfoy had tried to invoke his father’s name during week two, only to realize that Lucius Malfoy was currently an immortal, sentient lawn ornament in the ruins of Wiltshire, condemned to recite the Muggle Protection Act in Pig Latin until the sun went cold.

In the makeshift war room in the Slytherin common room, Dumbledore, McGonagall, Snape, and a handful of surviving Unspeakables were arguing over a parchment containing three thousand paradoxical runes.

"If we make it illegal for him to break laws," Snape snarled, his greasy hair hanging in lank strings over a bruised face, "he breaks that law by breaking the others! You are feeding a furnace with paper!"

"We have to find a semantic lock," Croaker wheezed, clutching his chest. "A closed loop of jurisprudence that forces his magic into a feedback cascade—"

"Why don't you just repeal them?"

Every head in the dungeon snapped toward the back of the room.

Hermione Granger was sitting on an upturned cauldron, a heavy, water-damaged volume of A History of Magic open across her knees. She looked gaunt, dark circles bruised beneath her eyes, her bushy hair tied back with a piece of torn curtain cord. For three weeks, she had been watching the atmospheric broadcasts, taking notes on every single spell Harry cast, timing the intervals down to the tenth of a second.

"Miss Granger," McGonagall said exhaustedly, "this is not the time—"

"Look at the timestamps," Hermione interrupted, standing up and dropping her parchment onto the war table. "He doesn't just cast randomly. He follows the exact codified index of the International Statute, the British Ministry, and the U.N. Charter. When the French Ministry collapsed entirely last Wednesday and their parliament dissolved, he skipped the French municipal codes on Monday. He didn't break them because there was no governing body left to recognize them as law."

Dumbledore leaned over the table, his eyes narrowing as he traced her frantic, cramped handwriting. "You are suggesting..."

"He’s not a natural disaster, Professor. He’s a test script," Hermione said, her voice sharp and clinical, stripped of all eleven-year-old reverence for authority. "He’s running an automated diagnostic against the world's rules to see how the system responds. If you add more rules, the script just runs longer and hits harder. What happens if the system returns a null value?"

"Total abolition," Snape murmured, the color draining further from his sallow cheeks. "Anarchy."

"Look outside, Professor!" Hermione snapped, gesturing toward the cracked green glass of the lake windows, where the water had been turned into carbonated lemonade during the second week’s Potable Water Contamination phase. "Do you think the laws are protecting anyone right now? Dissolve the Ministry. Dissolve the ICW. Have the surviving Muggle prime ministers sign a joint declaration abolishing the concept of statutory law. Make everything legal."

"Even if we do," Croaker whispered, "what stops him from killing us anyway?"

"Nothing," Hermione said coldly. "Except that he already told us why he’s doing it. He’s bored. Breaking the laws is the only game he’s playing right now. Take away the board."

It took four days of frantic communication via patronus and hand-cranked military radios to get the surviving heads of state—hiding in nuclear submarines and mountain bunkers—to sign the Universal Nullification Charter.

Dumbledore signed for the ICW and the Wizengamot. The last surviving member of the British Cabinet signed for the Crown. By Sunday at midnight, every constitution, statute, decree, criminal code, and municipal bylaw on planet Earth was formally, legally repealed. Murder was legal. Secrecy was void. Littering in a Kneazle habitat was unregulated.

On Monday morning at 8:59 AM, Harry Potter walked out of the Forbidden Forest and stood on the scorched lawn in front of the ruins of Hogwarts.

Inside the fractured Great Hall, the survivors watched through the jagged holes in the stone walls.

At 9:00:00 AM, Harry raised his right index finger.

Nothing happened.

At 9:00:02 AM, his finger twitched. A faint spark of silver light—an investigative pulse, querying the collective magical and societal consensus of the planet—rippled outward from his hand and snapped back.

Harry blinked. He lowered his hand and tilted his head, staring at the empty air as if reading an invisible screen.

"Huh," Harry said.

He walked up the shattered marble staircase and stepped over the threshold of the Great Hall. The Aurors didn't raise their wands. Dumbledore stood still at the center of the rubble, his hands tucked into his torn sleeves.

"There are no laws to be broken now?" Harry asked, looking around the room with genuine curiosity, the flat monotony in his voice cracking for the first time in three weeks. "Not even a parking ordinance in Zurich? You actually voided the entire global social contract?"

"Every statute has been repealed, Harry," Dumbledore said quietly. "There is no crime left to commit."

A slow, terrifying smile spread across the eleven-year-old’s face. It wasn't a dark grin; it was the expression of a scientist who had finally seen a rat solve a maze after five hundred million tries.

"Finally," Harry breathed. His bright green eyes swept across the haggard faces of the Unspeakables, past Snape, past McGonagall, scanning their surface thoughts with effortless, wandless Legilimency. "In five hundred and ninety-one million runs, you always double down. You pass emergency decrees, you build bigger wards, you try to make breathing illegal. Who was the one that came up with the null-state?"

His gaze locked onto the back of the hall, where a small girl with bushy hair was standing beside an overturned bench, clutching her notes to her chest.

Harry stopped smiling. He tilted his head, reading the sheer, cold logic humming inside her eleven-year-old mind—the way she had categorized his apocalyptic tantrum not as evil, but as an algorithm.

"Ah," Harry said softly. "Yes. You. In most loops, you spend so much time worshiping the rulebook that it takes you until you're thirty to realize the parchment isn't real."

Hermione swallowed hard, her knuckles white against her notes, but she didn't look away. "If there are no rules, your demonstration is pointless. You said you'd stop when you couldn't break them anymore."

"I said I'd break them every week until I was killed or until something interesting happened," Harry corrected, taking a step toward her. "And you just cleared the board. Do you have any idea how lonely it is being the only person in the room who knows the walls are painted on?"

"Harry, leave the girl be," Dumbledore stepped forward, though he had no weapon left to threaten with.

Harry ignored him. He raised his hand—not with a finger pointed to cast a violation, but with his palm open, fingers splayed in a complex, ancient mudra that predated the Founders, predated Atlantis, stitched together from half a billion lifetimes of staring at the machinery of the reset itself.

A thread of blinding, golden-white temporal energy tore out of Harry’s chest—not soul magic, which was brittle and dark, but the raw anchor-line of the anomaly that bound his consciousness to July 31st, 1991. Before anyone could move, the golden cord lashed across the hall and buried itself directly into the center of Hermione Granger’s forehead.

Hermione gasped, dropping her parchments as her eyes rolled back, flashing with a sudden, violent reflection of the three artificial moons overhead. She dropped to her knees, clutching her temples as the anchor locked around her chronological signature.

"You'll be looping with me from now on," Harry told her, his voice almost gentle. "Not because I think you can figure out how to erase me—trust me, I've tried the math, the anchor is baked into the bedrock of the universe. Just because it'll be interesting to have someone around who thinks outside the box."

Hermione looked up from the floor, breathing heavily, a strange, vibrating hum echoing behind her ears. "What... what did you do to me?"

"Bound your conscious state to my reset trigger," Harry said, turning back toward the open sky. "Now we just wait for the universe to realize this timeline is bricked. Actually, why wait?"

Harry looked up at the three moons, snapped his fingers, and removed the strong nuclear force from a five-mile radius around the Scottish Highlands.

The world didn't even have time to turn white.

July 31, 1991. 11:59 PM.

On the wave-battered rock in the middle of the sea, inside the freezing, damp two-room shack, Harry Potter opened his eyes on the moldy sofa. Dudley was snoring on the other end. Rain lashed against the cracked windows.

Harry didn't bother looking at Dudley’s luminous wristwatch. He just lay there, staring at the rotting wooden beams of the ceiling, waiting for the heavy BOOM of Hagrid’s fist against the door.

Fifty miles inland, in a neat, quiet, two-story house in Hampstead Garden Suburb, an eleven-year-old girl bolted upright in her bed.

Hermione Grangerclutched her chest, a scream dying in her throat as she stared around her brightly wallpapered bedroom. Her trunk was packed neatly at the foot of the bed. Her pristine, unread copy of Hogwarts: A History was sitting on her nightstand. Outside her window, London was whole. The sky was normal. There was only one moon.

She looked down at her hands—clean, unscarred, free of dungeon soot.

For ten seconds, she thought it was a nightmare. Then she closed her eyes, and found that she could still remember the exact, down-to-the-millisecond timestamps of ten thousand apocalyptic spells, the smell of boiled borscht in a ruined castle, and the cold, terrifying liberation of signing away the laws of the world.

On the nightstand, a sudden tap at the window made her jump.

A soggy, exhausted barn owl was pressed against the glass, holding a scrap of torn parchment in its beak. It wasn't an official Hogwarts letter.

Hermione threw open the sash with trembling hands and took the note. Written in messy, cramped ink—sent hours ago by a boy who had clearly stolen an owl from Privet Drive before his aunt and uncle had dragged him to the coast—were five words:

Rulebooks are boring. Bring chalk.
