Auracast Broadcast Audio Architecture for the Woojer Haptic Fleet

Technical Engineering Report on Overcoming Legacy RF Limitations via Bluetooth LE Audio and Low-Cost Hardware Sourcing
Author: Dillon Simeone (Deaf Lead Design Engineer)
Project Director: Shawn Trail, PhD
Project Administration: Tracy Held (CymaSpace / Cascade Pacific)
Hardware Scope: 60-Unit Woojer Vest Fleet Integration

1. Executive Summary and Problem Statement

Universal Music Design (UMD) maintains an inventory of 60 Woojer haptic vibration vests. These wearable devices represent one of our primary tools for providing real-time, bodily tactile listening during collaborative rehearsals, deaf jazz ensemble performances, and public community workshops.

However, our previous wireless broadcast setup faced severe operational bottlenecks that made large-scale group sessions unreliable, noisy, and expensive to expand. This report documents the research, architecture, and hardware bench testing undertaken by Lead Design Engineer Dillon Simeone to transition the entire 60-vest fleet from legacy radio systems to the modern Auracast Bluetooth Low Energy (LE) Audio standard.

Key Project Takeaway: By securing pocket-sized Auracast receiver units on AliExpress for approximately $10 each, UMD can achieve synchronized, ultra-low-latency stereo broadcast to all 60 vests simultaneously, eliminating analog radio static and replacing hard-to-source RF transmitters with a clean, modern solution.

2. Critical Limitations of Legacy Wireless Broadcast Systems

Prior to this research, our group rehearsals and workshop deployments relied on traditional analog radio frequency (RF) wireless transmitters and standard Bluetooth audio. Both architectures present fatal flaws for accessible multisensory music:

A. Legacy Analog FM/RF Transmitters (The 60-Vest Bottleneck)

  • Radio Static and Cross-Talk: In concert venues, academic halls, and urban studio environments, analog FM signals pick up severe radio frequency interference, wall reflection static, and electromagnetic hum from stage lights and synthesizers.
  • Mono-Only Limitation: Standard FM beltpack receivers provide only a single summed mono audio channel. This prevents deaf listeners from experiencing stereo panning, spatial room imaging, or directional separation across left and right chest actuators.
  • Cabling Bulk and Maintenance: Distributing audio to multiple FM transmitters required bulky 3.5mm splitter trees, delicate external antennas, and constant battery replacements.
  • Difficult Sourcing and Maintenance: Replacement analog RF transmitters and beltpack receivers are difficult to source and maintain, making ongoing repairs and fleet expansion challenging.

B. Classic Bluetooth Audio (A2DP / SBC / AAC)

  • Strict Connection Limits: Classic Bluetooth is architected exclusively for one-to-one (or at most one-to-two) point-to-point connections. It cannot broadcast to a classroom or ensemble of 60 vests simultaneously.
  • High Latency Timing Smear: Standard Bluetooth audio introduces between 150 and 250 milliseconds of transmission latency. For a deaf musician relying on physical vibrations to lock into a drummer's groove, a 200 ms delay completely destroys rhythm and makes synchronized playing impossible.

3. The Auracast Solution (Bluetooth LE Audio Broadcast)

Introduced as part of the Bluetooth 5.2 and 5.3 core specifications, Auracast broadcast audio fundamentally changes wireless audio topology by replacing point-to-point pairing with an open, connectionless broadcast stream.

Specification / Parameter Legacy Analog FM Systems Classic Bluetooth (A2DP) Auracast (Bluetooth LE Audio)
Simultaneous Receivers Multiple (degrades with range) 1 or 2 maximum Unlimited (60+ vests effortlessly)
Transmission Latency Low (0 to 5 ms) High (150 to 250 ms) Ultra-Low (10 to 20 ms via LC3)
Audio Channel Separation Mono only Stereo (point-to-point) Discrete True Stereo & Multi-Stream
Signal Quality & Interference Static, hiss, RF crosstalk Digital compression artifacts Crystal-clear digital LC3, zero hiss
Hardware Sourcing & Availability Difficult to source, custom RF hardware Common consumer standard (1-to-1) Accessible ~$10 AliExpress units

Key Technical Capabilities for UMD:

  1. Unlimited Concurrent Listeners: Auracast uses Broadcast Isochronous Streams (BIS). The master audio transmitter broadcasts packets into the air, and any number of receivers in the room can tune in simultaneously without establishing a handshake or consuming transmitter bandwidth. All 60 Woojer vests receive the exact same audio stream at the exact same instant.
  2. Low Complexity Communication Codec (LC3): Auracast utilizes the LC3 codec, which delivers higher fidelity audio than legacy SBC at half the bitrate, while keeping broadcast latency between 10 and 20 milliseconds. This latency is imperceptible to the human body, allowing deaf musicians to feel transient drum strikes in real time.
  3. True Stereo Torso Spatialization: Auracast transmits discrete left and right audio channels. By feeding stereo sound into the Woojer vest, wearers feel directional motion across their chest, giving cochlear implant recipients and deaf participants a spatial sense of where instruments are located on stage.

4. Low-Cost Hardware Sourcing and Bench Testing Results

Dillon identified, secured, and bench-tested compact, battery-powered Auracast receiver units sourced directly on AliExpress for approximately $10 each.

Bench Testing Observations:

  • Sub-Bass Frequency Response: Bench tests verified that the ~$10 receivers cleanly reproduce sub-bass frequencies from 10 Hz to 250 Hz without low-end roll-off or digital high-pass filter cuts. This is critical for tactile transducers, which operate primarily in the 20 Hz to 120 Hz band.
  • Clean Transducer Drive: When connected directly to the Woojer 3.5mm auxiliary input, the receiver's line-out signal provides ample gain (+0 dB) without clipping, distortion, or background hiss.
  • Battery Life: The self-contained lithium-ion battery in each dongle delivers over 6 hours of continuous listening, exceeding the duration of any standard rehearsal or community workshop.
  • Interference Immunity: Testing within an active studio environment confirmed zero dropout or RF crosstalk, even when passing through studio walls and proximity to high-power Wi-Fi routers.

5. Next-Generation Custom Silicon (ESP32-S31)

In addition to testing off-the-shelf receivers, Dillon coordinated with an engineering colleague visiting hardware markets in Shenzhen, China, to obtain cutting-edge ESP32-S31 microcontroller units supporting the Bluetooth LE Audio Auracast broadcast specification.

This research establishes an open hardware pathway for UMD to build dedicated, customized transmitters. By streaming digital audio directly over USB from a digital audio workstation (DAW) into an ESP32-S31, UMD can broadcast multi-channel tactile mixes without needing intermediate digital-to-analog converters.

6. Conclusion and Rollout Plan for UMD

The transition to Auracast solves one of Universal Music Design's most persistent logistical hurdles. By pairing affordable ~$10 receiver units with future custom ESP32-S31 hardware, UMD can outfit its entire 60-vest fleet with static-free, low-latency, stereo tactile audio.

This innovation will be rolled out across upcoming deaf jazz rehearsals, public school workshops, and live community performances, ensuring that every participant experiences the full physical power of music with perfect timing and clarity.
